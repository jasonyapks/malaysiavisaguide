## Extended stay length

Taiwan first introduced a six‑month digital nomad visa in 2025 for remote employees and owners of overseas businesses. In January 2026 the government officially lengthened the scheme, allowing the visa to be held for up to two years. The permit is issued for an initial six‑month period and can be extended three times, each extension lasting six months.

The two‑year window gives nomads ample time to experience Taiwan’s varied attractions, from the night markets of Taipei to the coastal charm of Taitung and the marble cliffs of Taroko National Park. It also provides a stable base for those who wish to maintain a long‑term remote‑work routine while exploring the island.

## Income and financial proof

Financial eligibility is split by age. Applicants aged 20 to 29 must demonstrate a minimum annual income of $20,000, while those 30 and older need to show at least $40,000 per year. In addition, candidates must provide bank statements proving an average balance of $10,000 over the previous six months, alongside proof of remote employment and international health insurance.

Compared with many golden‑visa programmes that demand high investment or income levels, Taiwan’s thresholds are relatively modest. The lower financial bar is intended to attract a broader pool of digital professionals without compromising fiscal safeguards.

## Application process and alternatives

Prospects complete the application on a dedicated portal, then print, sign and deliver the documents to the nearest Taiwanese embassy. Authorities typically review submissions within seven to ten days before issuing a decision. The short processing time is designed to accommodate the fast‑moving nature of remote‑work lifestyles.

For remote workers seeking a longer‑term pathway, Taiwan also runs a “Gold Card” talent visa. This three‑year programme bundles a work permit, resident visa and re‑entry permit, costs between $100 and $310, and targets sectors such as education and biotechnology. Gold Card holders may apply for permanent residency after three years, with family members eligible for dependent or visitor visas.
