## The official version, finally

Ask an agent, a travel blog and a forum thread how Malaysia's eVisa works, and you'll likely get three different answers. Each tends to leave something out, and the forum post was often out of date before anyone replied to it.

On 18 November, Malaysia's Immigration Department set the rules out itself, in a series of notices on its official Facebook page. They all point to one portal: [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my). Apply there and nowhere else, and check the address before you pay anything.

## Who has to apply

The department lists 30 nationalities: Afghanistan, Angola, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Burkina Faso, Burundi, Cameroon, the Central African Republic, Colombia, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the Republic of the Congo, Djibouti, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Ghana, Guinea-Bissau, India, Côte d'Ivoire, Liberia, Mali, Montenegro, Mozambique, Nepal, Niger, Nigeria, Pakistan, Rwanda, Serbia and Sri Lanka.

The footnotes matter as much as the list. Indian citizens appear on it, yet they're visa-exempt until 31 December 2026. Afghan citizens coming on a social visit need a visa approval letter before they can apply at all.

Myanmar sits under a separate note on ASEAN travellers. Citizens of the other ASEAN countries need no visa for a stay of under a month. Stay longer and they do, unless they hold a Brunei or Singapore passport. Myanmar citizens get no such allowance: they need a visa even for a shorter stay.

## The air-only rule most people miss

Twenty of the listed countries, all in Africa, carry an air-only mark. On one of those passports, you can't cross the causeway from Singapore, come over the Thai border, or arrive by ferry. You have to land at an airport.

That trips up travellers who fly into Singapore for the cheaper fare and plan to take the bus up to Johor Bahru. Check your passport against the official list before you book.

## What it costs and how long it takes

You'll pay the visa fee for your nationality, which varies from country to country, plus a RM105 processing fee and a 0.8% eCommerce fee. Pay by AliPay, WeChat Pay or UnionPay and an eWallet fee of 1.7% applies.

Your application isn't decided by a desk in Kuala Lumpur. It goes to the Immigration Attaché or Malaysian representative office that covers your country, and the department says that takes two to seven working days. Count weekends and public holidays in both countries, and two weeks before your flight is the safer margin.

An approved visa is valid for six months for social visits, students and expatriates, and three months for foreign workers. Carry the eVisa note with you, printed or on your phone, because you'll need to show it at the entry point.

## What to have ready before you start

The portal asks you to scan and upload a recent studio passport photo and the biodata page of your passport. That passport must be valid for at least six months from your travel date. You'll also need a confirmed return ticket, proof of where you're staying, and any other supporting documents your case calls for.

Notice the order. The ticket and the accommodation come first, before you apply, not after approval. If your dates might move, pay for fares and rooms you can change or cancel.

## Coming to look before you move?

Many people visit Malaysia before applying for MM2H or PVIP. They view property, meet a bank, or find out whether Penang suits them better than Kuala Lumpur. If your passport is on this list, that trip is a social visit like any other and runs on an eVisa, so plan it the same way.

The long-term pass is a separate application, made through its own channel. Entry rules for every nationality are on the [Immigration Department's website](https://www.imi.gov.my).
