## Nomads at the limit can stay on

Malaysia will let digital nomads who have used up their DE Rantau Nomad Pass stay longer. In his Budget 2027 speech in the Dewan Rakyat on 9 October 2026, Prime Minister and Finance Minister Anwar Ibrahim said existing holders who reach the pass's two-year limit will be allowed to continue staying and working remotely in Malaysia, subject to the prescribed eligibility criteria.

The measure was the second of six initiatives the speech grouped under making it easier for global companies to operate in Malaysia and bring in the best skilled talent. Others include a 5% tax rate for new Global Services Hub companies and work rights for the spouses of Category I Employment Pass holders.

## How the pass works today

The DE Rantau Nomad Pass is administered by the Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation (MDEC) and issued by the Immigration Department as a Professional Visit Pass. It is granted for 3 to 12 months and can be renewed once for a further 12 months, so a nomad can currently stay for up to about two years before having to leave the programme.

Applicants in digital technology roles must show an annual income of at least USD24,000. Non-tech professionals must show at least USD60,000. Work must be for an employer or clients outside Malaysia. The pass does not allow the holder to take up local employment.

## What changes, and what is still unknown

The announcement removes the hard stop at the end of the renewal for nomads who meet the criteria. That matters for people who have settled into a life in Kuala Lumpur, Penang or Johor Bahru, have children in school or have signed long leases.

The speech did not say how long the further stay will be, what the eligibility criteria are, what it will cost, when it starts or how holders apply. Several news outlets reported the extension as two more years, but that figure does not appear in the speech. The details normally follow from MDEC and the Immigration Department after the budget is tabled, and may also appear in the budget's annexes.

## What it means if you are considering Malaysia

For remote workers already in Malaysia, this is a clear signal that the government wants to keep them. For those weighing Malaysia against other digital nomad destinations, the prospect of staying beyond two years makes the country more competitive.

The DE Rantau pass is still a remote-work pass, not long-term residency. If you expect to make Malaysia your long-term base, buy property or retire, compare it with Malaysia My Second Home (MM2H) or the Premium Visa Programme (PVIP) before you commit.
