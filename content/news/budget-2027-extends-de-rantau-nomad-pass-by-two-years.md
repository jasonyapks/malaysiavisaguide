## A further two years for nomads at the limit

Malaysia will let digital nomads who have used up their DE Rantau Nomad Pass stay longer. In his Budget 2027 speech in the Dewan Rakyat on 9 October 2026, Prime Minister and Finance Minister Anwar Ibrahim said existing holders who have reached the pass's two-year limit will be allowed to stay and work remotely in Malaysia for an additional two years, subject to conditions.

The measure was one of six initiatives in the budget aimed at attracting global companies and skilled talent. Others include a 5% tax rate for new Global Services Hub companies and work rights for the spouses of Category I Employment Pass holders.

## How the pass works today

The DE Rantau Nomad Pass is administered by the Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation (MDEC) and issued by the Immigration Department as a Professional Visit Pass. It is granted for 3 to 12 months and can be renewed once for a further 12 months, so a nomad can currently stay for up to about two years before having to leave the programme.

Applicants in digital technology roles must show an annual income of at least USD24,000. Non-tech professionals must show at least USD60,000. Work must be for an employer or clients outside Malaysia. The pass does not allow the holder to take up local employment.

## What changes, and what is still unknown

The announcement roughly doubles how long a nomad can stay in Malaysia, from about two years to about four. That matters for people who have settled into a life in Kuala Lumpur, Penang or Johor Bahru, have children in school or have signed long leases, and until now faced a hard stop at the end of their renewal.

The speech did not set out the conditions, the fees, the start date or how holders apply. It also did not say whether the extra two years will be granted in one go or as further renewals. Those details normally follow from MDEC and the Immigration Department after the budget is tabled.

## What it means if you are considering Malaysia

For remote workers already in Malaysia, this is a clear signal that the government wants to keep them. For those weighing Malaysia against other digital nomad destinations, a possible four-year stay makes the country more competitive for people who want to stay longer than a year or two.

The DE Rantau pass is still a remote-work pass, not long-term residency. If you expect to stay in Malaysia for longer than four years, buy property or retire, compare it with Malaysia My Second Home (MM2H) or the Premium Visa Programme (PVIP) before you commit.
