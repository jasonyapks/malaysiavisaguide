## Top of a 26-city list

Penang has collected another international accolade. A new study by Click Intelligence, a UK digital marketing agency, compared 26 cities in 15 countries on what they cost digital nomads to live in. Penang came first with an overall score of 0.78, which made it the most affordable city in the study. The researchers estimate one person can live there for about €850 (roughly RM3,860) a month, and that figure includes rent.

The study came out in October 2026, and the US personal-finance site Moneywise reported on it on 26 October. It weighed several factors to judge how appealing each city is to remote workers. These were monthly living costs for one person, rent, internet access, safety, and whether the country offers an official digital nomad visa.

Penang scored 0.78 out of a possible 1. Busan in South Korea came second with 0.72, and Kuala Lumpur was third with 0.59. The rest of the top ten were Fukuoka, Seoul, Tartu in Estonia, Chiang Mai, Valencia, Osaka and Brno in the Czech Republic.

## What the money covers

The study puts the rent for a one-bedroom flat near Penang's city centre at about €393 (around RM1,785) a month. That leaves roughly €457 (about RM2,075) for food, transport, internet and leisure.

James Owen, Click Intelligence's co-founder and director, told Moneywise that cost isn't the only reason Penang works for remote workers. English is widely spoken, and Malaysia is generally seen as a safe place to live. Both make it easier for foreigners to settle in.

He said most digital nomads in Penang end up living in George Town. It's walkable, full of history and culture, and has plenty of cafés and co-working spaces.

Malaysia's DE Rantau digital nomad pass also gives eligible foreign remote workers a legal way to live and work in the country. The pass lets qualifying applicants stay for 12 months, and they can apply to extend it.

## Wong Hon Wai: Penang is more than just cheap

Penang's state executive councillor for tourism and the creative economy, Wong Hon Wai, shared the study on Facebook. He called Penang's top spot another encouraging piece of international recognition.

He said Penang's edge goes beyond its affordable cost of living. It also has good connectivity, a safe place to live, and the convenience of Malaysia's digital nomad pass.

He added that Penang's mix of cultural heritage, internationally known food, a growing arts and creative scene, and beautiful natural scenery makes it a place to live, work, travel and explore. George Town's status as a UNESCO World Heritage city gives the state its own historical and cultural character.

In Wong's view, the study shows Penang's growing international appeal. He also sees it as recognition of the state government's ongoing work to develop tourism and the creative economy.
