## Government crackdown on SVP abuse

At a Cabinet meeting on 30 January, Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim urged authorities to clamp down on foreign nationals who exploit Social Visit Passes to take up employment. The directive was reiterated by Communications Minister Datuk Fahmi Fadzil at his daily press briefing, where he warned that the misuse of passes intended for tourism or family visits undermines immigration policy.

Fahmi, also serving as Home Affairs spokesperson, said the Prime Minister’s concern stemmed from recent reports and a briefing that highlighted a surge in illegal work activities among visitors. He stressed that enforcement will be intensified and that any element of employment discovered on a social pass will trigger immediate action, signalling a tougher stance by the MADANI Government.

## Enforcement figures reveal scale

Home Minister Datuk Seri Saifuddin Nasution Ismail disclosed that 54,791 foreign nationals faced enforcement action in 2025 and up to mid‑January 2026. Of this total, 51,100 cases were recorded during the 2025 calendar year, while an additional 3,691 violations were logged in the first half of 2026.

The figures, presented during the same Cabinet session, underscore a persistent pattern of abuse despite previous visa‑relaxation measures. Officials indicated that the enforcement data will guide future policy adjustments and that the government remains committed to safeguarding local employment opportunities.

## Implications for foreign visitors

For individuals holding a Social Visit Pass, the crackdown means stricter monitoring of activities and a lower tolerance for any form of paid work. Visitors are now advised to verify that their stay complies strictly with the pass conditions, as violations can lead to detention, fines or deportation.

Employers who engage foreign workers on a social pass also face penalties, including revocation of business licences and possible criminal charges. The heightened enforcement aligns with the broader MADANI agenda to tighten immigration controls and protect the domestic labour market.

## Future policy outlook

Analysts suggest that the government could revisit the criteria for issuing Social Visit Passes, potentially introducing higher financial thresholds or stricter eligibility checks. Such adjustments would aim to deter individuals seeking to bypass the work‑visa system while preserving legitimate tourism and family visits.

The Home Ministry has indicated that public education campaigns will accompany enforcement, warning both visitors and employers of the legal consequences. Continued reporting from agencies such as the Immigration Department is expected to keep the crackdown transparent and data‑driven.
