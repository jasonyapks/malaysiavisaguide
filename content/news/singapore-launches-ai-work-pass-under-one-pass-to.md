## Purpose and Scope

The Singapore government announced a dedicated AI work pass designed to attract senior specialists in artificial intelligence, advanced computing and quantum technologies. The move responds to a noticeable shortfall in locally trained expertise as firms in finance, logistics and advanced manufacturing accelerate AI adoption. By providing a clear immigration route, the authorities hope to bring in the depth of knowledge required to scale national projects.

The AI track sits within the existing Overseas Networks and Expertise (ONE) Pass, which was first introduced in 2022. It supersedes the earlier Tec Pass programme and offers a five‑year stay that can be extended for an additional five years. The extended tenure is intended to give companies sufficient time to integrate foreign talent and facilitate knowledge transfer to the domestic workforce.

## Eligibility Criteria

Applicants must be currently employed, or have most recently been employed, by a technology company, a technology division of a larger organisation, or a technology‑focused venture capital firm. The focus is on individuals who can demonstrate expertise in the strategic areas identified by Singapore, namely AI, advanced computing and quantum research. The pass does not open to general workers; it is narrowly aimed at top‑tier specialists.

In line with the broader ONE Pass rules, candidates are expected to earn a minimum of S$30,000 per month, a threshold set when the scheme was launched in 2022. This salary floor ensures that only highly paid professionals, typically with exceptional achievements, are considered. The government has not disclosed any additional points‑based scoring system for the AI track.

## Implications for Businesses

Enterprises facing an AI talent crunch can now petition the Ministry of Manpower for a pass on behalf of a qualified candidate. The streamlined ONE Pass framework reduces processing time compared with older work‑visa routes, allowing firms to fill critical roles more quickly. Companies are also expected to support the professional’s integration, as the policy aims to blend imported expertise with local up‑skilling programmes.

While the AI pass offers a clear pathway for foreign specialists, policymakers stress that it complements, rather than replaces, domestic talent development. Prime Minister Lawrence Wong highlighted the dual strategy of attracting global expertise while nurturing home‑grown skills. Companies will therefore need to balance hiring from abroad with continued investment in training and education for Singaporean workers.
