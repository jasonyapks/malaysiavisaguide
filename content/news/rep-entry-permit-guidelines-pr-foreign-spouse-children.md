KUALA LUMPUR — That family route now has a written roadmap. The Immigration Department of Malaysia (JIM) and TalentCorp have jointly published guidelines on applying for an Entry Permit under the REP category. These spell out each step, document and processing time from the Long-Term Social Visit Pass through to the MyPR card.

The booklet was developed with the Ministry of Human Resources (KESUMA), the Home Ministry (KDN) and the National Registration Department (JPN). Until now, fast-track PR for REP dependants was promoted as an incentive, but there was no single published procedure applicants could follow.

## Who REP is for

To qualify, a Malaysian must have worked and lived abroad continuously for at least three years and earned no income in Malaysia for at least 36 months. They must also apply before returning and have no outstanding scholarship or loan bond with the government. TalentCorp has run the programme since 2011; it was introduced in 2001.

On top of the mandatory criteria, TalentCorp assesses each applicant holistically. It looks at qualifications; overseas income, with more weight above RM20,000 a month; occupation, including critical roles on the MyCOL list; and work experience abroad and in Malaysia.

Approved participants can be considered for four incentives: a 15% flat tax rate on employment income for five years; tax exemption on personal effects brought home; an excise duty exemption of up to RM100,000 on one locally assembled car; and PR for a foreign spouse and children under 18. The new guidelines deal only with the last of these.

## Who can apply for PR

Two groups are eligible: the foreign spouse of the REP participant, and the participant's biological children who hold foreign citizenship and are under 18.

The marriage must have lasted a minimum period before an application is accepted: three years for a foreign wife and five years for a foreign husband.

Timing matters. Dependants can only apply after the REP participant has returned to Malaysia, taken up a job in their approved field and received a Surat Akuan Tarikh Kembali (SATK) from TalentCorp. The application must also be made while the REP approval letter is still valid, which is normally two years. Applicants need a passport valid for at least 12 months and two Malaysian referees aged 21 or above.

## How the process works

First, the applicant uploads documents to TalentCorp's MyHeart portal for an initial check. With TalentCorp's support letter in hand, the applicant and sponsor then go to JIM headquarters in Putrajaya to apply for a 12-month Long-Term Social Visit Pass. There is no interview. Once the documents are complete, JIM says it will decide within one working day. Dependants who already hold a valid spouse or child pass can skip this step.

Next comes the Entry Permit application. Every case goes through a police security check and needs the Director-General of Immigration's approval. JIM aims to decide complete applications within four months of issuing the acknowledgement of receipt. The approval letter is valid for six months. After approval, the permit itself is issued within one working day. The holder then has 30 days to apply for the MyPR card at JPN, which takes about two months.

The Entry Permit has no expiry date. Holders can live, work, study and run a business in Malaysia without switching passes. Subject to state rules they can own property, and if eligible they can later apply for citizenship. They cannot vote or join political parties, and they must not stay outside Malaysia for more than six months at a time. Permits issued in Peninsular Malaysia or Labuan are valid only there. Sabah and Sarawak decide their own applications.

## Fees and deposits

The Long-Term Social Visit Pass costs RM90 a year and the Entry Permit RM120 a year, payable by card only. A security deposit is also required, set by nationality: RM200 for Singaporeans, RM1,000 for Japanese and South Koreans, RM1,500 for Chinese nationals, and RM2,000 for Americans and Canadians.

The deposit can be reclaimed after the permit has been held for two years.

Rejected applicants can appeal to the Home Minister within 30 days. Anyone who doesn't appeal, or loses an appeal, must wait a year before applying again.

## Why it matters

In a written parliamentary reply in October, KESUMA said 5,234 Malaysians had returned home through REP between 2011 and August 2026. Over that period TalentCorp received 12,850 applications and approved 8,093. The government says the clearer family procedure is meant to give skilled Malaysians more confidence to return with their families.

For foreign spouses, though, the route is narrow. It depends entirely on a Malaysian partner who qualifies for REP and returns on its timeline. Families who fall outside those rules, such as a couple married too recently or a spouse who has already moved back, will need to look at other long-term options. These include the [Premium Visa Programme (PVIP)](/visas/pvip/) and [Malaysia My Second Home (MM2H)](/visas/mm2h/).
