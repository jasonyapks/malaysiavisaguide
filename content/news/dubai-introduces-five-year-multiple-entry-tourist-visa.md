## Visa scope and validity

The new tourist visa issued by the United Arab Emirates is valid for five years and permits the holder to enter the country repeatedly without re‑applying. Each entry can last up to 90 days, while the total stay across a calendar year cannot exceed 180 days. The allowance is designed for visitors who need regular access for personal or professional reasons.

By allowing unlimited trips within the five‑year window, the scheme removes the administrative burden of frequent visa renewals. Travelers can schedule business meetings, family gatherings or leisure trips with greater certainty, while the 90‑day per‑visit cap maintains a balance between openness and regulatory oversight. The policy aims to stimulate repeat tourism and related spending.

The policy is positioned as a tool to boost tourism revenue by encouraging longer stays and repeat visits. Analysts expect that the ability to remain for up to three months per entry will attract conference delegates and shoppers, while the annual cap ensures that the visa remains a short‑term instrument rather than a de‑facto residency route.

## Eligibility and application process

The General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs (GDRFA) announced the visa as part of its effort to simplify entry for global tourists. While the exact eligibility thresholds are not detailed in the release, applicants must satisfy the standard security and health checks that apply to all UAE visitors.

Applications are submitted through the official digital portal, where users upload required documents and pay the visa fee online. The government advises candidates to confirm the current fee schedule before payment, as charges may vary. Processing times are described as swift, though the source does not give a specific timeframe.

## Implications for frequent travellers

For individuals who travel to the UAE several times a year—such as expatriates with family ties, consultants, or event organisers—the five‑year multiple‑entry visa removes the need to apply for a new visa before each trip. This continuity can reduce administrative costs and provide greater predictability when arranging flights and accommodation.

The 90‑day per‑visit limit and the 180‑day annual ceiling mean that visitors must still monitor their cumulative stay, but the flexibility to exit and re‑enter without fresh paperwork supports longer business projects and seasonal family visits. The programme also signals the UAE’s intent to attract high‑value repeat tourism, which may influence comparative decisions with other long‑stay visas such as Malaysia’s MM2H.
