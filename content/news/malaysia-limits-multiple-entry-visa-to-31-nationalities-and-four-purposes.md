## The visa that made scouting trips easy

Almost nobody moves to Malaysia sight unseen. You come once to work out whether Penang suits you better than Kuala Lumpur. You come again to view property or sit down with a bank. Sometimes there's a third trip to sign. If your passport needs a visa, the Multiple Entry Visa used to cover all of that in one application.

Since 1 September 2026, it doesn't, at least not for a holiday or a look around. [VisasNews](https://visasnews.com/en/malaysia-limits-multiple-entry-visa-to-31-nationalities-and-four-travel-purposes/), reporting the Immigration Department's announcement, says the MEV "will no longer be available for all nationalities." It is now limited to citizens of 31 countries, and to four reasons for travelling.

## The four purposes that still qualify

- Business
- Fly & Cruise, where you fly in and join a cruise
- Medical treatment
- Wedding tourism

Choose "Multiple Entry Visa" on the MyVISA portal with an eligible passport and those are the only four options the form gives you. VisasNews and Action-Visas confirmed this with test applications on 4 September.

General tourism has gone, and so have ordinary visits to family or friends. If that's why you're coming, you need a Single Entry Visa, one for each trip.

## Which 31 countries?

This is the awkward part: the department hasn't published the list. [iVisa](https://www.ivisa.com/news/2026-09-02-my-multiple-entry-visa-31-nationalities) advises checking with your nearest Malaysian embassy or consulate, or on the MyVISA portal itself.

The portal tests do point to a likely answer. It's the same 30 nationalities in the Immigration Department's [eVisa guide](/news/malaysia-evisa-who-needs-one-fees-and-processing-time/), plus Myanmar: Afghanistan, Angola, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Burkina Faso, Burundi, Cameroon, the Central African Republic, Colombia, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the Republic of the Congo, Côte d'Ivoire, Djibouti, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Ghana, Guinea-Bissau, India, Liberia, Mali, Montenegro, Mozambique, Myanmar, Nepal, Niger, Nigeria, Pakistan, Rwanda, Serbia and Sri Lanka.

Use it as a strong guide, not an official list.

## How long the visa lasts

Each entry lets you stay up to 30 days, and iVisa says that can't be extended. The visa itself has been valid for up to six months, a concession under the Visa Liberalisation Plan that started on 1 December 2023. According to iVisa, that concession runs to 31 December 2026. Nobody has said yet whether it will be renewed or go back to three months.

Applying after the new year? Check the validity on the portal before you plan trips around it.

## Who this affects, and who it doesn't

**You hold a visa-exempt passport.** Nothing changes. These rules only touch nationalities that need a visa.

**You're on the list and travelling for one of the four reasons.** You can still get an MEV. A business owner with meetings in Kuala Lumpur every few months is exactly who it still serves.

**You're on the list and coming to look around.** Most future MM2H and PVIP applicants are in this group. Plan each trip as its own single-entry application. And don't assume a property viewing, a bank appointment or the medical exam for your pass counts as business or medical treatment. Ask before you apply under either.

**You're Indian.** Your 30-day exemption ends on 1 January 2027, under an Immigration Department notice dated 17 November, [The Week reports](https://www.theweek.in/news/world/2026/11/19/no-more-visa-free-entry-for-indians-as-malaysia-revises-rules-key-dates-for-travellers.html). Enter on or before 31 December 2026 and you can still use it. After that you'll need a visa, and because India is on the likely list of 31, an MEV will only be possible for those four purposes.

## Where to apply

Use the official portal, [malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my](https://malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my), and nowhere else. Neither report gives the MEV fee, so check what applies to your nationality on the portal before you pay.

The visa only covers your visit. The long-term pass you're working towards is a separate application, made through its own channel.
