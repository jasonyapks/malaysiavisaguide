## Approval numbers

Sarawak’s tourism, creative industry and performing arts minister Abdul Karim Rahman Hamzah told the state legislative assembly that 560 applications were approved last year. A further 265 were cleared by mid‑May this year, taking the total past 800 since the scheme began. Bernama reported the figures from the sitting.

Karim said the state is now looking to Japan, after a rise in Japanese visitors. The decision rests with applicants, he said, but the pattern matches interest from Europe and China, where Sarawak is seen as safe and well served by hospitals.

## Economic impact of deposits

Approved applicants have placed RM176.9 million in fixed deposits with banks in Sarawak, according to the minister. The money is locked for the life of the visa.

Karim called the deposit requirement a core part of the programme. Every visa holder puts capital into the state directly, on top of what tourism and other sectors bring in.

## Education and new target markets

Schooling is a large part of the draw for foreign families. International schools across Sarawak run curricula that suit expatriate children, and Karim named education as a main reason recent applicants came forward.

The state now plans to market the MM2H programme harder in Japan, on the back of the growing tourist traffic. Pairing the visa with schools and hospitals is how Sarawak hopes to keep approvals climbing.
