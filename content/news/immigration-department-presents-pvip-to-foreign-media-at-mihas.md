## Immigration takes PVIP to the foreign press

If you've been weighing a long stay in Malaysia, you've probably heard PVIP described a dozen different ways, usually by someone selling it. On 25 September the Immigration Department of Malaysia (JIM) described it themselves.

JIM officers Tuan Asrul and Tuan Adib held a press conference at MIHAS to introduce the Premium Visa Programme to members of the foreign media. Industry representatives were also there, among them Jason Yap, director of the PVIP agency MYPVIP.

## What JIM put on the board

The programme's banner at the event listed the core terms. A PVIP pass is renewable and runs for up to 20 years. Holders are permitted to reside, study, work and conduct business activities in Malaysia, and they can invest as well.

The pass also covers family. You can bring your spouse and children, and your parents and parents-in-law too.

## Why the work rights matter

The work and business rights are the part people usually get wrong. Many long-stay routes into Malaysia let you live here but not earn here, and that includes the Silver and Gold tiers of MM2H. PVIP holders can work and run a business on the pass itself, with no separate approval.

The parents-in-law point comes up less often, but it settles the question for a lot of families. If moving means leaving your spouse's parents behind, most other routes make you choose. This one doesn't.

## Is it the right route for you

PVIP is aimed at high-net-worth individuals and their families, and the cost reflects that. If you only need a place to retire and won't be earning locally, MM2H may do the job for less. If you plan to work, build a business, or bring three generations with you, PVIP is the route built for that.
