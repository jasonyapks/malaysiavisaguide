## A webinar for founders weighing PVIP

If you're thinking about moving to Malaysia and starting a company here, you've probably found that the visa and the business get explained separately, by people who sell one or the other. A free webinar on 1 November sets out to cover both in one session.

The PVIP agency [MYPVIP](https://mypvip.com/) is hosting "From PVIP Visa to Business Owner: Unlocking Malaysia's Business Opportunities" on Zoom on Sunday 1 November 2026 at 8pm Malaysia time. MYPVIP appears on the Immigration Department's list of approved PVIP agencies. The session is in English, free to join, and ends with a Q&A.

The speakers are two MYPVIP business associates: PVIP consultant Chee Mee Ho, and Datin Jiniy Seelan, managing partner of JS Partners Chartered Accountant.

## What it says it will cover

The advertised agenda runs from an overview of the Premium Visa Programme and its benefits, through Malaysia as a regional business hub and the practical steps to incorporate a company, to growing the business once it is registered and a roadmap from visa to operations.

## What to know before you join

The premise holds. PVIP holders may live, work and run a business in Malaysia on the pass itself, without a separate work permit and without their company sponsoring them. That is the main reason founders look at PVIP rather than MM2H, whose Silver and Gold tiers do not allow work.

PVIP is also expensive, and an agency that sells PVIP applications has a reason to lead with it. The usual alternative for a founder is an Employment Pass issued through their own company. That route needs no fixed deposit or participation fee, but the Immigration Department expects a 100% foreign-owned company to hold RM500,000 in paid-up capital before it can employ a foreigner, including its owner, and RM1,000,000 in wholesale, retail and trade.

Neither route is right for everyone. Go in knowing the two numbers, ask how each applies to your business, and check what you hear against the official terms. The PVIP guide linked at the end of this article sets out the fees, the income requirement and the timeline.
