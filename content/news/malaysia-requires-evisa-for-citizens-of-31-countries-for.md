## New eVisa rule overview

The Malaysian government announced that, starting in 2026, citizens of 31 identified countries must secure an electronic visa before travelling for social purposes such as tourism, family visits or short‑term events. The move is presented as part of a broader effort to tighten border control and enhance national security while still encouraging legitimate travel.

The rule targets the Single Entry Visa (SEV) and ties eligibility directly to the traveller’s nationality. Applicants are required to complete the eVisa process online prior to departure, and the visa cannot be issued from within Malaysia, Israel or North Korea, meaning those locations are excluded from the application pipeline.

## How the single entry visa works

The SEV permits one entry into Malaysia for the approved purpose and is valid for a single trip only. Prospective visitors must submit supporting documents such as a valid passport, travel itinerary and proof of sufficient funds through the official eVisa portal, then wait for electronic approval before boarding their flight.

In contrast, the Multiple Entry Visa (MEV) is open to all nationalities except citizens of Israel and North Korea. Holders may enter Malaysia several times, but each visit is capped at 30 days and the passport must remain valid throughout the stay. The MEV therefore offers greater flexibility for repeat travellers who are not subject to the new SEV requirement.

## Implications for travellers and visa planners

For applicants from the 31 listed nations, travel planning now needs to accommodate the extra lead time required for eVisa approval. Failure to obtain the SEV before departure will result in denial of entry at the border, making early application essential, especially during peak tourism periods.

Other visa categories, such as the Visa With Reference (VDR) for work or study, continue to operate under existing rules. The earlier exemption for 15 African countries also remains unchanged, meaning those travellers are still not required to obtain a VDR regardless of purpose.
