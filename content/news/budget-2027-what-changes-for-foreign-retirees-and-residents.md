## No change to MM2H or PVIP

Malaysia's Budget 2027 speech, delivered by Prime Minister and Finance Minister Anwar Ibrahim on 9 October 2026, contained no announcement on Malaysia My Second Home (MM2H), Sarawak MM2H or the Premium Visa Programme (PVIP). Fixed deposit levels, participation fees, property requirements and minimum-stay rules were not mentioned and remain as they are.

The only residence passes named in the speech were the DE Rantau Nomad Pass and the Employment Pass, which we have covered separately. For retirees and long-stay residents, the measures that matter are in the tax, healthcare and tourism sections instead.

## Income tax for foreigners who are tax residents

A foreigner who spends 182 days or more in Malaysia in a year is generally a tax resident and pays the same progressive rates as a Malaysian on income that is taxable here, such as a local salary, rent from Malaysian property or Malaysian business income. Budget 2027 cuts two of those rates by one percentage point. Chargeable income from RM70,001 to RM100,000 will be taxed at 18%, and from RM100,001 to RM150,000 at 24%. The individual relief that every resident taxpayer receives, unchanged since 2010, rises from RM9,000 to RM12,000.

At the top, the speech said the rate for high-income earners with income exceeding RM1 million will be adjusted to 30%. Under the current schedule the 30% rate starts above RM2 million, so a resident with Malaysian income between those two figures should expect to pay more.

The speech did not mention the flat rate paid by non-residents, and it did not mention foreign-sourced income. The exemption for foreign-sourced income received by resident individuals, which is why most MM2H and PVIP holders pay no Malaysian tax on overseas pensions and investments, continues to 31 December 2036 under the existing order.

## Elderly care becomes cheaper

From 1 January 2027, the service tax rate on elderly care services will fall from 8% to 6%. Elderly care services with annual fees of up to RM96,000, which is RM8,000 a month, will be exempt from service tax altogether.

The speech set no nationality condition on this measure, and service tax is charged on the service rather than on the customer. Foreign retirees in private assisted-living or nursing facilities should therefore see lower bills, but how the exemption threshold is measured will depend on the regulations that follow. The government will also fund training for 8,000 elderly care workers and draft a Senior Citizens Bill.

## Private hospital bills and a new basic health plan

Beginning in 2027, private hospital billing will adopt standardised and transparent fee categories, so that patients can understand each charge. Most foreign residents use private hospitals and pay either out of pocket or through private insurance, so itemised and comparable bills are a practical gain.

The government will also launch a basic health plan called MediAsas from January 2027, under which hospital charges are based on diagnosis and treatment categories. The speech did not say whether non-citizens can buy it. Until that is clarified, MM2H and PVIP holders should keep the private medical cover they already have.

## Property and stamp duty

First-home buyers will get a full stamp duty exemption on homes priced up to RM500,000, and a partial one up to RM750,000, for sale and purchase agreements executed from 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2030. Earlier first-home exemptions were limited to Malaysian citizens, and the speech did not say this one will be different.

The speech announced no change to the stamp duty that non-citizens pay on residential property transfers, to Real Property Gains Tax, or to the minimum purchase prices that states set for foreign buyers.

## Forest City family offices

For Forest City, the speech confirmed that the Single Family Office scheme allows one family to manage its funds in Malaysia with zero tax on qualifying income for up to 20 years. A Multi-Family Office model will now be introduced, allowing licensed fund managers to serve several families, including approved single family office funds. This is aimed at wealthy families who want a Malaysian base without setting up a full office of their own. No start date or conditions were given.

## Travel, tourism and duty-free shopping

The Visit Malaysia Year campaign is extended through 2027, with RM935 million allocated for tourism and culture. A matching grant will support new direct flight routes from China, India, Central Asia and Europe, which should mean more ways for residents and their visiting families to reach Malaysia.

On the duty-free islands of Langkawi and Labuan, the duty-free purchase limit for visitors and tourists will rise to RM10,000, subject to a minimum stay of 48 hours. New body and luggage scanners will be installed at the Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Kota Kinabalu international airports.

## What it means if you are considering Malaysia

For someone weighing retirement or long-term residence in Malaysia, Budget 2027 leaves the visa routes alone and trims some of the costs around them. The tax changes are modest for most foreign residents, because overseas income remains outside the Malaysian net. The elderly care and hospital billing measures matter more to retirees planning for later life.

None of this is in force yet. Plan on the current rules, and check each measure again once the Finance Bill and the supporting orders are published.
