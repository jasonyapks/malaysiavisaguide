## Proposed limits on bridge financing

The Department of Homeland Security released a draft on 2 July that would sharply restrict the use of bridge loans in EB‑5 projects. Under the proposal, any financing that does not generate immediate, traceable employment would be excluded from counting toward the programme’s job‑creation requirement, a departure from past practice where pre‑development costs were permissible.

Attorney Jill Jones of JTC Group told Bisnow that the new definition could eliminate most upcoming EB‑5 offerings, as many projects rely on bridge debt to cover entitlement or architectural work that creates few direct jobs. She warned that the standard would raise the compliance burden and reduce flexibility for both developers and immigrant investors.

Since 1990, EB‑5 investors have contributed nearly USD 60 billion to US developments, according to Invest in the USA. The draft’s bridge‑financing ban threatens to cut off a key source of early capital, potentially slowing the flow of foreign money into new urban and mixed‑use projects.

## New capital deployment and timeline rules

The updated proposal requires EB‑5 applicants to fully deploy the required investment before the formal visa process can begin, ending the common practice of making partial upfront commitments. This shift forces investors to commit larger sums earlier, favouring those with deeper pockets.

In addition, the draft caps redevelopment project timelines at three months, a stark reduction from the current “commercially reasonable” one‑year expectation. The rule replaces case‑by‑case flexibility with a uniform enforcement framework, limiting developers’ ability to adjust schedules to market conditions.

## Potential impact on developers and investors

Industry observers say the combined restrictions could bottleneck the EB‑5 pipeline, with fewer projects meeting the tighter job‑creation and timing criteria. Smaller regional centres may struggle to compete with larger, better‑capitalised players, potentially reducing the diversity of projects that attract foreign capital.

With a 60‑day public comment window, many regional centres are reportedly accelerating offerings to lock in investments before the rules take effect. If the draft becomes final, developers may adopt more conservative funding strategies, and the US construction sector could see a contraction of the roughly USD 60 billion that has historically been supported by EB‑5 capital.
