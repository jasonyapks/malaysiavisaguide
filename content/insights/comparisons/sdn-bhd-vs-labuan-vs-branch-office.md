Ask four advisers how to set up in Malaysia and you'll get four structures. One says Sdn Bhd. One says Labuan, and mentions 3% tax in the first sentence. One says your overseas company should just open a branch. One suggests a representative office "to start".

They can't all be right for you, and you've likely noticed that each adviser recommends the thing their firm sets up. The structures aren't four versions of the same product. Each exists for a different kind of business.

**The short answer:** if you'll sell to customers in Malaysia or need to live and work here, set up a Sdn Bhd. A Labuan company suits a business whose customers are outside Malaysia and that can put a real office and staff in Labuan. A branch suits an established foreign company on a specific project. A representative office can't earn money at all.

## The four, side by side

| | Sdn Bhd | Labuan company | Branch of a foreign company | Representative office |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **What it is** | A Malaysian private company | A company under Labuan's separate regime | An extension of your overseas company | A liaison presence, no business of its own |
| **Registered with** | SSM | Labuan FSA, through a Labuan trust company | SSM | MIDA approval |
| **Government fee to set up** | RM1,000 | Varies by activity | RM5,000 to RM70,000, by the parent's share capital | None of the same kind |
| **Can earn income in Malaysia** | Yes | Designed for business with non-residents | Yes, in permitted activities | No |
| **Tax** | 24%, or 15% and 17% on the first RM600,000 if not more than 20% foreign-owned | 3% of audited net profits on trading activity, if substance rules are met | Malaysian tax on Malaysian income | None, as it has no income |
| **Liability** | Limited to the company | Limited to the company | The foreign parent is liable | The foreign parent is liable |
| **Retail, trading, restaurants** | Yes, with the trade ministry's approval | No | No | No |

## Which business is yours?

### You'll sell to Malaysian customers, or work here yourself

Set up a **Sdn Bhd**. It's the structure Malaysian banks, landlords, licensing authorities and the Immigration Department all expect. It can hold local licences, hire staff, and sponsor an Employment Pass once it meets the paid-up capital requirement.

Its weakness is tax. A company that is more than 20% foreign-owned pays 24% from the first ringgit, without the small-company rates. That's the price of operating in the domestic economy, and no structure on this page avoids it for Malaysian-source business.

The full first-year bill is in [what it costs to set up a company in Malaysia](/insights/how-to/company-setup-cost-malaysia-foreigner/).

### Your customers are all outside Malaysia

This is who a **Labuan company** is for. Labuan is a federal territory with its own company and tax regime, aimed at international business. A Labuan company carrying on a trading activity is taxed at 3% of its audited net profits. One that only holds investments is taxed at zero.

Now the part the pitch tends to skip. That rate is conditional. To keep it, the company must meet Labuan's substance requirements: a physical office in Labuan, an adequate number of full-time employees there, and an adequate level of annual operating spending in Labuan, with the numbers prescribed by type of activity. For an ordinary trading company these are commonly quoted as two full-time employees and RM50,000 a year, but check the current regulations for your activity. Miss them and the 3% is gone.

So the real cost of a Labuan company isn't the tax. It's an office and two salaries on an island you may never visit. For a business making good profits from overseas clients, that's cheap. For a freelancer invoicing a few clients, the substance costs more than the tax it saves.

Where it disappoints: it's the wrong tool for a business with Malaysian customers, it needs an audit every year, and banks look harder at Labuan entities than at ordinary companies.

### You're an established company abroad with a project in Malaysia

A **branch** lets your existing company operate in Malaysia without creating a new one. You register it with SSM and appoint an agent in Malaysia who answers for its compliance. The fee depends on your company's share capital: RM5,000 where it's not more than RM1,000,000, rising in steps to RM70,000.

The reason to choose it is credibility and simplicity on a defined project, typically where a client or tender wants to contract with the parent.

Where it disappoints: there's no liability shield, so a claim against the branch is a claim against your home company. And a branch can't do retail, trading or restaurants, because any business with foreign equity in distributive trade has to be locally incorporated.

### You're still deciding whether Malaysia is worth it

A **representative office** lets a foreign company put people on the ground to research the market, find suppliers and coordinate for head office. It needs MIDA's approval, is funded from abroad, and must spend at a minimum level each year, which MIDA's guideline has set at RM300,000.

Where it disappoints: it cannot trade. No invoices, no contracts in its own name, no revenue. If you find a customer, you can't serve them until you set up something else. It's for corporations studying a market, not for founders starting a business.

## The decision, in order

1. **Who pays you?** If any meaningful part of your revenue comes from Malaysia, it's a Sdn Bhd.
2. **Do you need to live and work in Malaysia?** A Sdn Bhd is the straightforward sponsor for an Employment Pass. Or hold a pass in your own right; see [which Malaysian visa lets you run a business?](/insights/comparisons/which-visa-lets-you-run-a-business/)
3. **Is everything offshore, and is the profit large enough to carry an office and staff in Labuan?** Then Labuan is worth pricing properly.
4. **Are you a company, not a founder, here for one project?** Consider a branch.
5. **Are you only looking?** A representative office, or just a few visits.

Don't start from the tax rate. A 3% structure you don't qualify for is a 24% structure with extra paperwork.
