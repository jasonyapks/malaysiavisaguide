You have been given two sets of numbers with the same three letters on them, and they do not sit anywhere near each other. One agent quotes you a deposit in US dollars and a compulsory house. Another quotes you half a million ringgit, no property at all, and sounds faintly like they are describing a different country. Neither of them is lying to you.

Here is the thing that resolves it, and almost nobody leads with it. **These are not two tiers of one programme. They are two programmes run by two governments.** Federal MM2H is administered by {{mm2h-silver:authority:text}} in Putrajaya. S-MM2H is administered by {{smm2h:authority:text}}. Sarawak runs its own immigration control, so it runs its own long-stay scheme, on its own application system, with its own sponsor rule and its own fee schedule. Nothing carries over. Once you see them as separate schemes that collided over a name, the two quotes stop contradicting each other.

> The state programme is not the budget version of the federal one. It is cheaper on capital and stricter on who you have to be — and it buys you residence oriented around one state rather than the whole country.

## The reversal nobody quotes at you

Federal MM2H has **no published income requirement on any tier**. Not on Silver, not on Platinum. Sarawak MM2H does: {{smm2h:incomeRequirement:moneyPer}} of pension or offshore income, rising to RM15,000 with a dependant, evidenced over the latest three months.

That is backwards from how people expect a state programme to behave, and it is the single most common reason a retiree gets a surprise late in the process.

Two corrections before you use it, because the headline is misleading in both directions.

**Sarawak's test has a second door.** Savings of RM100,000 — RM200,000 with a dependant — qualifies instead of the monthly income. That is a very different bar from RM10,000 a month, and it is left out of most write-ups, which quote the income figure alone and send qualifying applicants away.

**Federal's "no income test" does not mean bring nothing.** {{mm2h-silver:incomePractice.note:text}} _{{mm2h-silver:incomePractice.attributionBy:text}}, as at {{mm2h-silver:incomePractice.attributionAsAt:date}}. MOTAC's guide publishes no income figure either way, so this is practice rather than a published rule._

## Side by side, against the tier you would actually be choosing between

Sarawak's closest federal comparator is Silver — the entry tier, the same five-year first term, the same kind of applicant. Gold and Platinum are in a different capital bracket entirely.

Read the property row before the deposit row. The deposits are close enough that the currency they are denominated in is arguable — Silver's pledge is a US dollar sum whose ringgit size keeps moving after you commit, Sarawak's does not. The purchase is not arguable. Silver obliges you to buy a home from {{mm2h-silver:propertyPurchaseMin:money}}, and in the two states most applicants actually buy in, the binding floor is higher still: RM1,000,000 in Kuala Lumpur and RM2,000,000 in Selangor. Sarawak obliges you to buy nothing, ever.

That single row is most of the cost gap between these two programmes.

## Which one is yours

### You want Kuching, Miri or Kota Samarahan

Sarawak, straightforwardly, and you should be pleased about the numbers. You are choosing the programme built for where you want to be, and it happens to be the one that locks up far less of your capital. No purchase, a ringgit deposit, half of it accessible after a year, and a stay requirement of {{smm2h:minStayShort:text}} that you will clear without thinking about it.

### You want Kuala Lumpur, Penang or Johor

Federal, whatever the price difference. This is the case where the cheaper programme is simply the wrong product. The Sarawak stay requirement is 30 days **in Sarawak** — months in Kuala Lumpur do not count towards it — and the programme's work rights, its property floors and its sponsor all sit inside one state's system. Buying S-MM2H because it costs less, when your life is going to be on the peninsula, means paying less for something you did not want.

### You want to work part-time

Sarawak beats federal Silver and Gold outright here, which surprises people. {{mm2h-silver:workRights:text}} is the federal position on both of those tiers — barred, not restricted. Sarawak permits 20 hours a week in four named sectors, plus a minority stake in a joint venture. The only federal tier that permits work is Platinum, at a {{mm2h-platinum:participationFee.principal:money}} participation fee and a {{mm2h-platinum:fixedDeposit:money}} deposit.

If a part-time teaching or clinical role is part of your picture of retirement, that comparison is not close.

### You are asset-rich and income-light

Check the savings door before you rule Sarawak out. RM100,000 in savings, or RM200,000 with a dependant, qualifies in place of the monthly income — a low bar for the profile that usually gets turned away on the RM10,000 figure. If even that does not work, federal is the side with no published income test at all.

### You are bringing older children or your spouse's parents

Federal. Sarawak takes children up to 21; federal takes them up to 34 while unmarried, which is most of a decade of difference for a family with adult children. Federal also admits parents-in-law, and Sarawak's dependant list does not name them. For a multi-generational move, check the family list before the fee schedule.

### You are under 30

Federal, by default. Sarawak's minimum age is {{smm2h:minAge:number}}; federal's is {{mm2h-silver:minAge:number}}. There is nothing to weigh here.

Sarawak charges no participation fee at all — its state charges are a {{smm2h:processingFee.principal:money}} processing fee and a government-fixed {{smm2h:agencyFee.principal:money}} agency fee. ## Where each one disappoints people

**Sarawak** is procedurally heavier than its price suggests. You cannot apply alone — a sponsor or licensed agent has to bond you and file through the state system. Your medical has to be done in Sarawak and endorsed by a government doctor, so a report from home gets sent back to be redone before the pass is released, which is a second trip most people did not budget for. Local medical insurance is required. And the term is shorter than it reads: five plus five, then a fresh application rather than a renewal.

**Federal Silver** disappoints on the purchase and on the work bar. The RM600,000 headline is the national minimum, not the price you will be allowed to buy at, and applicants who assumed Kuala Lumpur discover the RM1,000,000 state floor late. The deposit sits in US dollars, so the ringgit size of your commitment moves with a rate you do not control. And no work of any kind is permitted — a retiree who imagined some consulting on the side has bought the wrong tier and will need Platinum for it.

## The fork, stated plainly

Answer the geography question first, and answer it honestly rather than aspirationally. If your life is going to be in Sarawak, take S-MM2H: it asks for less capital, forces no property purchase, permits part-time work that federal Silver forbids, and its stay requirement is the mildest of the two. If your life is going to be on the peninsula, federal is the only one of the two that is actually pointed at it, and the extra cost buys the right jurisdiction rather than a better deal.

Only if you are genuinely indifferent about where in Malaysia you live does this become a money question — and then Sarawak wins it, on the property row alone.
