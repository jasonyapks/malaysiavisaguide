"Move to Penang, it's cheaper." If you've spent any time on expat forums, you've read it a hundred times. Usually it's said by someone who loves Penang, to someone who hasn't asked what they'd give up.

The people who regret their choice of city rarely got the price wrong. They chose on price, then discovered the city didn't fit their life. They found they needed a hospital, an airport or an office that was in the other one. So here's the price, honestly. Then the question that should actually decide it.

**The short answer:** Penang and Johor Bahru both cost markedly less than Kuala Lumpur. The average household spends about RM5,900 a month in each, against RM8,178 in Kuala Lumpur. For a family, the biggest difference by far is school fees, which in Penang are around a third of Kuala Lumpur's for a young child. For a retired couple, Penang saves roughly RM1,500 to RM2,000 a month.

## What the average household spends

The Department of Statistics' 2024 survey measures spending by state. Here are the three side by side:

| Monthly, RM | Kuala Lumpur | Penang | Johor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Average household spending** | **8,178** | **5,850** | **5,927** |
| Median household spending | 6,789 | 4,860 | 5,280 |
| Housing, water and power | 2,497 | 1,427 | 1,328 |
| Eating and drinking out | 1,346 | 1,178 | 938 |
| Food at home | 976 | 680 | 975 |
| Transport | 868 | 521 | 735 |
| Everyday spending, excluding housing, transport, health, education and insurance | about 4,100 | about 3,300 | about 3,300 |
[^1]: Department of Statistics Malaysia, Household Expenditure Survey 2024, Tables 1.3 and 3.1. Figures cover all households in each state, most of them Malaysian. The housing line includes the imputed rent of owner-occupiers. Johor is the whole state, not Johor Bahru alone.

Two things stand out. Penang households spend far more eating out than on food at home, even more than Kuala Lumpur does in proportion, which fits Penang's reputation as a food city. And everyday spending outside housing is about **RM800 a month lower** in Penang and Johor than in Kuala Lumpur. That's the saving you get just by living there, before rent or schools.

Staple food prices are close across all three. In October 2026 the trade ministry recorded a median of RM9.39 for a kilo of chicken in Kuala Lumpur, RM9.19 in Penang and RM8.78 in Johor. The difference is in how you live, not in what the market charges.

## Rent: what the same flat costs in each city

From a sample of listings read on 23 October 2026, these are median asking rents in the areas foreigners most often choose:

| Area | 2 bedrooms | 3 bedrooms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kuala Lumpur:** Mont Kiara | 3,000 | 4,300 |
| **Kuala Lumpur:** Bangsar | 3,900 | 4,200 |
| **Penang:** Gurney Drive | 5,500 | 7,000 |
| **Penang:** Tanjung Tokong | 2,700 | 2,000 |
| **Penang:** George Town and Pulau Tikus | 2,500 | 2,400 |
| **Penang:** Batu Ferringhi | 1,500 | 2,400 |
| **Johor:** Johor Bahru city | 2,500 | 2,800 |
| **Johor:** Iskandar Puteri | 2,300 | 2,900 |
[^2]: RM a month, median asking rents, PropertyGuru, read 23 October 2026. Asking rents, not agreed rents. In some areas three-bedroom units list for less than two-bedrooms because the larger units are in older buildings.

Penang isn't cheaper everywhere. **Gurney Drive**, the seafront strip, lists three-bedrooms at a median of RM7,000, more than Mont Kiara. But step a few kilometres away, to Tanjung Tokong, George Town or the beach at Batu Ferringhi, and the same space costs about half. Johor Bahru is consistently lower than Kuala Lumpur, without Penang's premium enclave.

## School fees: the biggest difference of all

For a family, nothing else on this page comes close.

| Annual tuition 2026/27, RM | Year 1 / Grade 1 | Year 7 / Grade 7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Garden International School, Kuala Lumpur | 85,590 | 118,170 |
| Uplands, Penang | 31,800 | 71,400 |
| Dalat International School, Penang | 31,800 | 57,200 |
[^3]: Each school's published 2026/27 schedule, read 23 October 2026. Foreign families pay 6% service tax on annual fees above RM60,000.

A child starting primary school costs about **RM54,000 a year less** in Penang. With two children, that's more than the whole of the household budget of the average Penang family. We haven't priced Johor's international schools here. [International school fees in KL and Penang](/insights/expat-living/international-school-fees-kl/) covers four more schools and what your child's pass allows.

## Which city is yours?

**You're retired and want an easy, affordable life by the sea.** Penang. A two-bedroom near the beach or the old town plus everyday spending comes to roughly RM5,500 to RM6,500 a month, against RM7,500 to RM8,500 for the same life in Mont Kiara. Before you commit, check how far you'd be from the hospital you'd actually use, and how often you'll need to fly. Kuala Lumpur has more direct international flights.

**You're moving with school-age children and paying the fees yourself.** Penang, unless your work ties you to Kuala Lumpur. On school fees alone, the saving can pay the rent several times over.

**Your work or family is in Singapore.** Johor Bahru. It's across the causeway from Singapore, and many residents live in Johor and work or shop on the other side. Your budget is lower than in Kuala Lumpur on almost every line. If you're looking at MM2H, read about [the SEZ and SFZ route through Forest City](/insights/comparisons/mm2h-sez-sfz-forest-city-2026/), which is specific to Johor.

**You'll work, run a company, or need the country's widest choice of everything.** Kuala Lumpur. It costs more, and you're paying for access: to clients, to government offices, to the biggest hospitals and the main international airport. For many people that's worth the extra RM2,000 a month.

**You'd like the cheapest option of all.** Look beyond the peninsula. Sarawak runs its own version of MM2H, with lower capital and no compulsory property purchase. See [Federal MM2H vs Sarawak MM2H](/insights/comparisons/federal-mm2h-vs-sarawak-mm2h/).

One more thing to check before you choose: **property floors for foreign buyers are set by each state.** Kuala Lumpur's is RM1,000,000. Penang and Johor each set different floors for different areas and property types, and the sources we checked in October 2026 don't agree on the current figures. If you're on MM2H, where buying is compulsory, confirm the floor with the state land office before you look at a single property.

((Spending figures are DOSM's 2024 survey. Rents are asking rents from a dated listing sample, read 23 October 2026. School fees are each school's published 2026/27 schedule. Verify current figures before you commit.))
