The zone route is the part of MM2H that most comparisons leave out, and it is the cheapest way into the programme by a wide margin. The fixed deposit is less than half of Silver's, and less again from age 50. That is the number that brings people to it.

The number that should decide it is different. On this route you buy the property first, inside one designated zone, and you hold it for ten years. So the useful question is not whether you can afford the deposit. It is whether you would be content owning that particular property, in that particular place, for a decade, even if the application behind it had not yet been approved when you signed.

## What the SEZ and SFZ routes are

MOTAC's Guide: Malaysia My Second Home of December 2025 lists four categories: Platinum, Gold, Silver, and a combined Special Economic Zone / Special Financial Zone category. This site describes MM2H as three main tiers with two zone-based routes alongside them, because the zone route is not a cheaper rung on the same ladder. It is tied to a designated zone, its property floor is set by Johor's state property acquisition policy rather than by a national minimum, and it is approved in a different order. Everything else in this article follows from those three differences.

Forest City, the development in Johor, is the zone route most applicants meet. Its operator has published its own guidance for applicants, which we covered when it appeared: [Forest City's SFZ MM2H guidance and its lower deposit thresholds](/news/forest-city-sfz-mm2h-guidance-highlights-lower-deposit-thresholds/). That guidance explains how the programme is applied in the zone. It does not change MOTAC's rules, and the application still goes through a licensed MM2H agent to the One Stop Centre.

Three things stand out. The deposit is the smallest in the programme, and it is the only one that falls with age. The term is ten years, twice Silver's, for a participation fee the same as Silver's. And the minimum age is 21 rather than {{mm2h-silver:minAge:number}}, which makes the zone route the only MM2H option for an applicant in their early twenties.

What does not change is just as important. The stay requirement follows the same rule as the main tiers: {{mm2h-silver:minStayPerYear:text}} The ten-year hold on the property is the same. The agency fee is set by the government rather than by the agency, and the schedule prices the zone route with Silver; the [MM2H guide's fees section](/visas/mm2h/) lists it. And there are no work rights, which MOTAC's guide states in the same words it uses for Silver and Gold.

## The property comes first, and that is the real difference

On Silver, Gold and Platinum the order is approval, then endorsement, then a purchase completed within the following year. [How to apply for MM2H](/insights/how-to/apply-for-mm2h-2026/) sets that sequence out stage by stage. On the zone route the first two swap: the in-zone purchase is a condition of approval, so it is made before you hold one. ((MYPVIP practice, as at 23 August 2026))

The written sources do not all describe it that way. MOTAC's December 2025 guide words the purchase as following approval on every category, the SEZ/SFZ page included, and press summaries of Forest City's own guidance have described the same order. We are not going to resolve that from here. What matters to an applicant is that the two orders carry very different risks, and that you should know which one your application will actually follow before money changes hands.

If the purchase comes first, you are committing a property price to a single development before any decision on the visa has been made. A refusal does not unwind the purchase. On the main tiers the money at risk before approval is the share of the agency fee paid on submission; on the zone route it can be a property you bought for a reason that no longer exists.

{% callout tone="warning" title="Get the sequence in writing before you sign anything" %}
Ask whoever is handling the application to state in writing which comes first in your case, the purchase or the approval, and what happens to the purchase if the application is refused. If the answer is that the purchase comes first, read the sale agreement for what it says about a refused visa before you sign it, not after.
{% endcallout %}

The fixed-deposit withdrawal is affected too. MOTAC's guide allows up to 50% of the principal to be withdrawn for purchasing a residence, education, medical and tourism activities in Malaysia, and the SEZ/SFZ page carries that clause in the same words as the others. On the main tiers the window opens once the purchase completes, which follows approval. On the zone route the purchase precedes approval, so that rule does not carry across, and when the window opens here is a question we have not been able to settle. Budget as though the deposit and the property price must both be available in full at the same time.

## Who the zone route suits

The route makes sense for a fairly specific applicant.

- **Someone who wants the property anyway.** If you would buy in the zone without a visa attached, the purchase is not a cost of the visa; it is a decision you had already made, and the zone route is then the cheapest pass that comes with it.
- **An applicant under 25.** The main tiers start at {{mm2h-silver:minAge:number}}. The zone route starts at 21, according to MOTAC's guide.
- **An applicant aged 50 or over with limited liquid capital.** The deposit halves at 50, and MOTAC's guide applies its minimum-stay requirement only to participants below 50. The over-50 position across the programme is a subject of its own; the point here is only that the zone route is the cheapest entry for this group.
- **Someone who wants ten years without Gold's capital.** Silver runs {{mm2h-silver:tenureYears:years}}. The zone route runs ten for a far smaller deposit, at the cost of where you buy.

It suits less well if you want to choose where in Malaysia you own property, if you need to work or run a business locally, or if the ten-year hold on one development's property is something you would want to exit early. MOTAC's guide permits a sale within ten years only to upgrade to a residence of higher value, and says that failing to comply with any of the terms results in the pass being revoked.

## The catches

**The property floor is not a national number.** The guide ties the floor to Johor's state property acquisition policy and prints no figure. The about-RM500,000 starting point is practice, not the guide, and what you will pay is set by the development, not the programme. ((MYPVIP practice, as at 3 August 2026))

**You are exposed to one development's resale market for ten years.** On the main tiers you can buy anywhere that clears the state floor, which at least lets you choose a market you understand. On the zone route the location is fixed, and the ten-year hold means you cannot react to it.

**The order of events is contested in writing.** The practice and the published guide describe it differently, as set out above. That is reason enough to ask, not reason to assume.

**The withdrawal timing is unsettled.** The permitted uses are published; when the window opens on this route is not settled. Do not plan on reimbursing part of the purchase from the deposit until it is confirmed for your case.

**Renewal is cheap, but the conditions are not.** The pass runs ten years and is renewable. After the full term, MOTAC's guide sets the renewal fee at RM300 for the principal and each dependant, against RM1,500 on Silver, every five years. The property, stay and deposit conditions still have to be met when you renew. [MM2H renewal: requirements, fees and timing](/insights/how-to/mm2h-renewal-requirements-and-fees/) walks through what is checked.

**Scrutiny of the zone is not hypothetical.** In July 2026 the Immigration Department inspected foreign residents at Forest City and found that all 266 it checked held valid documents. We reported it as [Immigration says 266 foreigners in Forest City have valid documents](/news/immigration-says-266-foreigners-in-forest-city-have-valid/). Holders should expect the conditions attached to the pass to be checked, and should keep the evidence that they meet them.

> On the zone route the deposit is the small number. The purchase you make before approval is the one that decides it.

## Check the agent before anything else

The zone route goes through the same licensed agents as the main tiers. Ask for the agent's MOTAC licence number and check it yourself against [MOTAC's licensed MM2H company list](https://www.motac.gov.my/en/kategori-semakan-new/licensed-mm2h-company/). It matters more here than on the main tiers, because on this route you may be asked to commit to a property before any approval exists, and the person handling your application is the one you will rely on to explain what happens if it is refused.
