You've read the tier table three times. Silver, Gold, Platinum. Five, fifteen, twenty years. Small, medium and large deposits. It looks like a hotel loyalty scheme, and the natural reaction is to pick the middle one and feel sensible about it.

Here's what the table doesn't tell you: **on Silver and Gold, your life in Malaysia is the same.** Same right to live here, same family on the pass, same schools, same healthcare, same ban on working. Gold doesn't buy you a better MM2H. It buys you a longer one, and it charges for that in capital you can't touch.

**The short answer:** the three main tiers differ in only three ways: how long the pass runs ({{mm2h-silver:tenureYears:years}}, {{mm2h-gold:tenureYears:years}} or {{mm2h-platinum:tenureYears:years}}), how much capital you commit, and whether you can work. Only Platinum lets you work or run a business. Choose Silver to retire on the least capital, Gold for long-term certainty, Platinum only if you'll earn here.

## What's the same on every tier

This is the part most comparisons skip, and it's the part that should drive your decision. On Silver, Gold and Platinum alike:

- **Minimum age** is {{mm2h-silver:minAge:number}}. There's no upper limit.
- **Minimum stay** is 90 days a year if you're 25 to 49, and the days can be split between you and your family. From 50 there's no minimum stay at all.
- **Dependants** are the same list: your spouse, unmarried children up to 34, children with a medically certified disability at any age, and your parents and parents-in-law. The participation fee is per application, so they don't multiply it.
- **No income test.** {{mm2h-silver:incomePractice.note:text}} ({{mm2h-silver:incomePractice.attributionBy:text}}, as at {{mm2h-silver:incomePractice.attributionAsAt:date}}.)
- **The deposit rules.** {{mm2h-silver:fixedDepositWithdrawable:text}} The permitted uses are in MOTAC's guide; the timing is MYPVIP practice, as at 17 October 2026.
- **The property rules.** The purchase is compulsory, and you hold it for ten years unless immigration lets you sell to buy something of higher value.
- **Schools and healthcare.** Children can study up to university level, and long-term medical treatment in Malaysia is allowed.
- **The tax line.** MOTAC lists a tax exemption on incoming funds such as the deposit as a benefit of every tier. Tax rules move, and an MM2H pass doesn't decide your tax residency, so verify the current rules before you rely on it. [Is foreign income taxed in Malaysia?](/insights/expat-living/is-foreign-income-taxed-in-malaysia/) explains how it works.
- **If the principal dies,** the pass can transfer to the next of kin among your registered dependants.

Read that list again with Gold in mind. Nothing on it improves when you move up from Silver.

## Silver: the retirement tier, with one catch

Silver is the tier most people should start from. The fees for one person come to about RM41,000. The deposit is {{mm2h-silver:fixedDeposit:money}} and the property minimum is {{mm2h-silver:propertyPurchaseMin:money}}, though the state you buy in may set a higher floor.

**Silver suits you if** you're retiring, you won't work here, and you'd rather keep most of your capital working elsewhere. If you're over 50, the missing stay requirement means you can treat Malaysia as one home among several.

**The catch is the clock.** The pass runs {{mm2h-silver:tenureYears:years}}, but the property has to be held for ten. So at year five you renew while still bound to the house. MOTAC's guide says Silver renews every five years after its term, at RM1,500 per person plus the pass and visa fees for the new five years. A renewal is checked against the rules in force when you file, and you have to still meet the deposit, property and stay conditions on that day. [MM2H renewal requirements and fees](/insights/how-to/mm2h-renewal-requirements-and-fees/) goes through what's checked.

## Gold: you're paying for time, not for more

Gold runs {{mm2h-gold:tenureYears:years}}. That's its whole advantage over Silver. It costs about RM17,000 more in fees, the deposit goes up to {{mm2h-gold:fixedDeposit:money}}, more than three times Silver's, and the property minimum rises to {{mm2h-gold:propertyPurchaseMin:money}}.

Here's the honest arithmetic. Gold doesn't skip the five-yearly sticker renewal: the pass is still renewed every five years, or with your passport, with a pass fee and visa fee each time. What Gold saves you, on MOTAC's renewal table, is Silver's RM1,500 category fee at years five and ten. That's about RM3,000 per person over fifteen years. Nobody should choose Gold to save money.

**Gold suits you if** you're sure you'll still be here in fifteen years, you have the deposit sitting idle anyway, and you'd pay for not having to requalify twice. If the property you want is above RM1,000,000 regardless, because you're buying in Kuala Lumpur or Selangor, Gold's higher property minimum costs you nothing extra.

**Gold disappoints people who** expected it to unlock something. It doesn't let you work, invest or run a business. If that's what you hoped for, Gold is a more expensive Silver.

## Platinum: the only tier that lets you work

Platinum runs {{mm2h-platinum:tenureYears:years}}, and MOTAC's guide marks business, investment and career activity **Permissible**. On Silver and Gold all three are not allowed. Platinum is also the only tier that lets you bring a foreign domestic helper.

It's priced accordingly. The participation fee alone is {{mm2h-platinum:participationFee.principal:money}}, and it's gone once paid. Fees for one person come to about RM270,000. The deposit is {{mm2h-platinum:fixedDeposit:money}} and the property minimum {{mm2h-platinum:propertyPurchaseMin:money}}.

**Platinum suits you if** you'll earn, invest or run a company here, you can park a million dollars, and you want the property anyway. "Permissible" in the guide is a broad word, so ask exactly what your planned work or business would need before you build a plan around it.

**Before you pay {{mm2h-platinum:participationFee.principal:money}}, check the alternatives.** [MM2H Platinum vs PVIP](/insights/comparisons/mm2h-platinum-vs-pvip/) compares it with the other twenty-year route, which needs far less capital but tests your income. [Which Malaysian visa lets you run a business](/insights/comparisons/which-visa-lets-you-run-a-business/) covers the cheaper routes for people who mainly want to work.

## Which tier is yours?

**You're retiring, you won't work, and you want to keep capital free.** Silver. Plan for the year-five renewal from day one.

**You're retiring, you're certain about Malaysia, and your money is already sitting in cash.** Gold, if fifteen years without requalifying is worth the bigger deposit to you. If you're not certain, Silver costs you less to find out.

**You want to work, invest or run a business here.** Platinum is the only MM2H tier that allows it. Compare it with PVIP before you commit.

**You're not sure MM2H is the right programme.** Look outside the tiers first. [MM2H vs PVIP vs DE Rantau](/insights/comparisons/mm2h-vs-pvip-vs-de-rantau/) compares the main long-stay routes, Sarawak runs its own version on different terms ([Federal MM2H vs Sarawak MM2H](/insights/comparisons/federal-mm2h-vs-sarawak-mm2h/)), and the SEZ and SFZ zone routes are covered in [MM2H in the SEZ and SFZ](/insights/comparisons/mm2h-sez-sfz-forest-city-2026/).
