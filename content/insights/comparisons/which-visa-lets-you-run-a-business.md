You've found the visa you can afford and the business you want to start. Then you read the conditions and can't find a sentence that says you're allowed to do both.

Agents don't help much, because most are licensed for one programme and will tell you theirs is fine. The thing you've likely noticed is that the brochures talk about lifestyle, schools and golf, and go very quiet on the word "work".

**The short answer:** PVIP and MM2H Platinum let you work and run a business in your own right. An Employment Pass lets you work for one company, which can be your own if it holds the required capital. DE Rantau covers remote work for clients outside Malaysia only. MM2H Silver and Gold are for living in Malaysia, not for working here.

## Owning is not the same as working

Start here, because it clears up half the confusion.

Buying or holding shares in a Malaysian company is an investment. In most sectors a foreigner can own 100% of one without living in Malaysia at all. That needs no visa.

Working in the company is different. Sitting in the office, managing staff, signing contracts and drawing a salary is employment, and that's what your pass has to permit. So the useful question isn't "can I own a company on this visa?" It's "can I work in it?"

## Which founder are you?

### You want to run a real business and live here for good

Look at **PVIP** first. It isn't cheap, and it's the least restrictive pass on the list. You can work, invest and run a business, for {{pvip:tenureYears:years}}, and none of it depends on a company staying alive.

It also solves a problem most founders don't see coming. Every Malaysian company needs a director who lives in Malaysia. A PVIP holder who lives here is accepted as that director, so you don't pay for a nominee and the company doesn't need to sponsor anyone.

Where it disappoints: the deposit is large and it's not business capital. It sits in a bank while your company needs funding of its own.

### You want to run a business and have the capital for a higher MM2H tier

**MM2H Platinum** also carries full work and business rights. It comes with MM2H's other conditions, including a compulsory property purchase, so it suits someone who was going to buy a home in Malaysia anyway. We compare the two directly in [MM2H Platinum vs PVIP](/insights/comparisons/mm2h-platinum-vs-pvip/).

Where it disappoints: Platinum carries the largest fixed deposit in the table, several times PVIP's. If you were drawn to MM2H by the lower tiers' price, this isn't that.

### You're here for this one business

The **Employment Pass** is the standard route. Your company registers with the Immigration Department's Expatriate Services Division, shows the paid-up capital for its ownership structure, and employs you at a salary of at least {{employment-pass:salaryFloor:money}} a month for the lowest category.

It's the cheapest way in, because the capital is working money your business can spend. The details are in [paid-up capital for an Employment Pass through your own company](/insights/how-to/paid-up-capital-employment-pass-own-company/).

Where it disappoints: the pass is tied to that company. Close the business, sell it or fall out with a co-owner, and your right to stay goes with it. Your spouse and children are on dependant passes that hang off yours.

### You work online for clients abroad

**DE Rantau** fits a freelancer or remote employee whose income comes from outside Malaysia. It's cheap and quick by comparison.

Where it disappoints: it isn't a business visa. It doesn't allow local employment, so it won't cover you running a Malaysian company that sells to Malaysian customers.

### You're retiring, and the business is a side idea

**MM2H Silver or Gold** will let you live here comfortably. They aren't work passes. If the side idea turns into something that needs you in the office, you'll need a different pass before you start, not after.

## The mistake to avoid

The expensive mistake is choosing the pass on price, moving here, and then discovering the business you planned isn't something that pass lets you do. Changing passes later means a fresh application and, often, leaving and re-entering.

Decide in this order. First, will you personally work in a Malaysian business? If yes, your shortlist is PVIP, MM2H Platinum or an Employment Pass. Second, do you want your right to live here to depend on that business? If not, it's PVIP or Platinum. Only then compare the price.

For the company side of the decision, start with [what it costs to set up a company in Malaysia](/insights/how-to/company-setup-cost-malaysia-foreigner/).

Pass conditions change, and individual approval letters can carry their own terms. Read yours, and confirm with the issuing authority before you rely on it.
