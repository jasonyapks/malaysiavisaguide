**Answer first.** On a Dependant Pass alone, a spouse can't simply start work. Today they need a work endorsement from Immigration, or an Employment Pass of their own. From 1 January 2027, spouses of Category I Employment Pass holders are meant to be allowed to work, but the conditions haven't been published. Plan around today's rules until they are.

## The question every relocating couple asks second

The job offer comes first. Then, usually a day or two later, the other question: *and what about my partner's career?*

If you've searched for the answer, you've probably noticed that nobody commits. One page says a Dependant Pass holder can work. Another says they can't. A third says it depends on the employer. That isn't because writers are careless. It's because the rule has been a two-step one for years, with the second step handled quietly by Immigration, and the rule is about to change for one group of people.

Here is what the official sources actually say, and where they stop.

## What the rules say today

The Immigration Department's Dependant Pass FAQ lists the husband or wife of an expatriate as eligible for the pass, and says that spouse may work without having to change to an Employment Pass, "as long as he/she has received the endorsement for work from the Immigration Department of Malaysia."

Read that carefully. A Dependant Pass does not give the right to work. The *endorsement* does, and it is a separate permission, granted by Immigration once there's a job. The FAQ also expects a work contract stating the position, duration and salary, signed by employer and expatriate.

What we can't tell you is how readily the endorsement is granted at the moment. Practitioners and law-firm bulletins disagree on whether it is obtainable in practice or whether spouses are steered to a full Employment Pass, and we could not date or verify those accounts. That is why the first step for any couple is to ask the employer's HR team, in writing, which route Immigration is currently accepting.

## What Budget 2027 changes

In his Budget 2027 speech on 9 October 2026, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said that from 1 January 2027, spouses of expatriates holding a Category I Employment Pass who themselves hold a Dependant Pass will be allowed to work in Malaysia, subject to the prescribed conditions. [Our news story](/news/budget-2027-lets-employment-pass-spouses-work-in-malaysia/) has the full context.

Three things to hold onto:

- **It's Category I only.** Under the revised salary policy that took effect on 1 June 2026, Category I is RM20,000 a month and above. Spouses of Category II and III holders were not mentioned.
- **The conditions are unpublished.** As at 13 November 2026 we could not find which jobs, whether the employer must apply, or what it costs. The Expatriate Services Division is where details are likely to appear.
- **It starts in 2027.** Until then, nothing has changed.

## Which couple are you?

### Your partner is, or will be, Category I

You are the one this was written for. **Don't start a job on a Dependant Pass before the conditions are out.** If the start date matters, ask your partner's employer whether a spouse's work endorsement is available now, and if not, when they expect the new process to open. A senior hire negotiating an offer in the next few weeks can ask for the employer's commitment to support the spouse's application in writing.

### Your partner is Category II or III

The announced change doesn't reach you. Your options are the two that exist today:

1. **A work endorsement**, if Immigration is currently granting them, tied to a specific job.
2. **Your own Employment Pass**, sponsored by an employer. That has its own salary category, and the floor for Category III is {{employment-pass:salaryFloor:money}} a month. Your role has to clear it, and the employer has to be willing to sponsor you, which for many couples is the real obstacle.

### Your partner has been here three years and is on Category I

There is a third route most couples don't know about. The Residence Pass-Talent belongs to the principal, runs ten years, and its dependant pass lets a legally married spouse seek employment without a pass of their own. Our [comparison of the Employment Pass and the RP-T](/insights/comparisons/employment-pass-vs-residence-pass-talent/) sets out who qualifies. It has its own gates, including three consecutive years on an Employment Pass, and you have to apply yourself.

### You'd rather not depend on an employer at all

If a sponsoring employer is the thing you want to avoid, a capital route such as [PVIP](/visas/pvip/) allows the principal to work and run a business. Ask the agent in writing what the work rights are for each dependant before you assume they match the principal's.

## Where this disappoints people

- **It's a permission, not a right to any job.** "Subject to the prescribed conditions" can mean restrictions on sectors or a requirement for the employer to apply.
- **Your pass status is tied to your partner's.** If the principal's pass is cancelled or isn't renewed, a Dependant Pass doesn't survive it.
- **Tax follows work.** A spouse who earns in Malaysia has their own tax position. Verify the current rules with LHDN or an adviser before you start.

## What to do next

Write down your partner's Employment Pass category and basic monthly salary, and ask HR three questions in writing: is a spouse work endorsement available today, what is the process, and what will the employer support from 1 January 2027? Then read the [Employment Pass guide](/visas/employment-pass/) for how the new duration caps land on your category. If the answer to the first question is no, the second and third decide whether a sponsored Employment Pass or the Residence Pass-Talent is your route.

Rules for this group are changing, and the conditions are not yet public. Verify the current position with the Immigration Department or the Expatriate Services Division before you act; the dates on this page show when we last checked.
