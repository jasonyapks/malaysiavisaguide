Turning 50 changes one line of the MM2H rules, and it is the line most people over 50 were worried about. From that birthday there is no minimum number of days you have to spend in Malaysia each year. Everything else, from the deposit to the property, the fees and the paperwork, is the same as it is for a 30-year-old.

That makes MM2H a good fit for a retirement that is split between two countries. It also means that the decisions which actually cost money are the same ones a younger applicant faces: which tier, which state to buy in, and how many family members to bring.

## What changes for MM2H applicants over 50: the minimum stay

MOTAC's guide sets the stay rule by age rather than by tier, and applies it only to participants aged below 50 (p.19). In full: {{mm2h-silver:minStayPerYear:text}}

Two practical consequences follow for an applicant aged 50 and over.

- **A part-year life is allowed.** If you intend to spend the northern winter in Penang and the summer at home, or to come and go around family commitments, nothing in the pass requires you to count days.
- **Your tax position is a separate test.** Holding the pass does not make you a Malaysian tax resident, and having no minimum stay does not stop you becoming one. Residency is decided by how many days you spend here. We cover that in [the territorial rule and the 182-day test](/insights/expat-living/is-foreign-income-taxed-in-malaysia/).

## What does not change at 50

The absence of a stay requirement is easy to read as "MM2H can be run from abroad". It cannot. Several steps need you in Malaysia, in person, whatever your age.

1. **After approval, the in-country steps.** MOTAC's process chart has the applicant assemble the fixed deposit certificate, health insurance where it applies, the medical check-up report, a personal bond form and a valid passport within a fixed window after the approval letter, and then pay the fees and have the pass endorsed (p.23). The deposit has to be placed with a Malaysian bank and the medical taken at a panel clinic, so this is a trip, not a courier.
2. **The property purchase.** Buying a residence is compulsory on every tier after approval, and it may not be sold for ten years unless you are trading up to one of higher value (p.20). The purchase must complete within one year of the pass being endorsed. ((MYPVIP practice, as at 3 August 2026))
3. **Renewal.** In practice the renewal is submitted with you physically in Malaysia, because a copy of your latest Malaysian entry stamp is required, together with the latest fixed deposit balance and a medical check by a Malaysian clinic on Form MF II. ((MYPVIP practice, as at July 2026)) An applicant who uses the automated e-gates should raise the entry-stamp point early, since an e-gate entry does not produce a stamp.

The minimum age is also unchanged and, for an older applicant, irrelevant: MOTAC sets it at {{mm2h-silver:minAge:number}} for Silver, Gold and Platinum and states no upper limit (p.17).

## Which MM2H tier suits a retiree over 50?

For most retirees the choice is between Silver and Gold. Platinum's distinguishing feature is that it is the only tier on which you may work or run a business in Malaysia, and the only one that lets you bring a foreign domestic helper (p.20). If you are retired and do not need either, you are paying for permissions you will not use.

**Silver** is the lowest capital commitment and the shortest pass, at {{mm2h-silver:tenureYears:years}}. The point to plan around is that the property condition outlasts it: you must hold the residence for ten years, so a Silver participant who buys in the first year is committed to the property for longer than the first pass runs. That is a fine trade if you intend to renew. It is a poor one if you see MM2H as a five-year experiment.

**Gold** runs {{mm2h-gold:tenureYears:years}}, which covers the ten-year holding period with room to spare, and raises the deposit to {{mm2h-gold:fixedDeposit:money}}. For a couple in their fifties planning a long retirement, Gold's term fits the property obligation better than Silver's.

**Platinum** runs {{mm2h-platinum:tenureYears:years}} and carries a {{mm2h-platinum:participationFee.principal:money}} participation fee. It makes sense for an applicant over 50 who still intends to work, invest or run a company in Malaysia. For a retirement on its own, it is the most expensive way to buy rights you do not need.

The SEZ and SFZ zone routes are the one part of MM2H where the deposit itself is lower from 50. They tie you to buying inside a designated development, at that development's price, and their figures rest on practice rather than on MOTAC's category table. They are covered in [how to apply for MM2H in 2026](/insights/how-to/apply-for-mm2h-2026/).

## Bringing a spouse or parents on MM2H after 50

MOTAC's guide lets a participant bring a spouse, children within an age limit, and parents and parents-in-law, with no age limit stated for parents (p.20). For an applicant over 50 the spouse and the parents are usually the relevant names on the list.

What a dependant adds is fees, not capital. The guide charges no participation fee for dependants (p.18), and the deposit and property are requirements of the participant. Each dependant adds:

- a processing fee of {{mm2h-silver:processingFee.dependant:money}};
- a pass fee of {{mm2h-silver:passFeePerYear.dependant:money}} for each year of the approved term, paid at the approval stage rather than year by year;
- a multiple-entry visa fee set by the dependant's nationality, and a small one-off security bond;
- the compulsory medical check-up at a panel clinic after approval (p.19), and health insurance where the age rule requires it.

The government-fixed agency fee already covers the first dependant. From the second dependant onwards a further agency charge applies per person. ((MYPVIP, from the government MM2H fee schedule, as at 28 July 2026))

One provision is worth knowing for a couple. On the death of the principal, the guide makes the pass transferable to the next of kin among the registered dependants (Silver p.36). A spouse who is on the original application as a dependant is therefore in a materially better position than one who was left to apply separately.

## Medical insurance and the medical check-up after 60

Two health requirements apply, and they are often confused.

- **The medical check-up is compulsory at every age.** MOTAC requires it for the principal and every dependant, at a panel clinic or hospital appointed by the ministry, after approval (p.19).
- **Health insurance is required for applicants aged 60 and below.** That is how MOTAC's document list words it (p.23), and the insurer may be Malaysian or foreign provided the cover is worldwide. An applicant over 60 falls outside that requirement at the application stage.

Two cautions apply to the second point. The guide's renewal list asks for health insurance without repeating the age qualifier (p.24). And it does not say in terms whether the age test applies to each dependant separately or to the principal's age. If the principal is over 60 and the spouse is not, settle in writing at the start which of you needs cover, and at which stage.

## Do you need a pension or income to qualify?

There is no income requirement on any MM2H tier, which is one of the main reasons the programme suits retirees whose wealth is in savings and property rather than in a monthly figure. That is a published position, and it is also easy to over-read.

For a retiree, the practical answer is to attach pension statements and recent bank statements anyway. They are not scored, but they answer the question an officer is entitled to ask.

## A worked example: a retired couple on Silver or Gold

Take a couple: the principal is 66, the spouse 63, both living on pensions and savings. They want to spend about five months a year in Malaysia and the rest at home, and they plan to buy an apartment. Neither will work. The spouse joins as a dependant, and no one else is on the application.

| **Agency fee, covering the spouse as first dependant** | {{mm2h-silver:agencyFee.principal:money}} | {{mm2h-gold:agencyFee.principal:money}} |
| **Spouse's processing fee** | {{mm2h-silver:processingFee.dependant:money}} | {{mm2h-gold:processingFee.dependant:money}} |
| **Spouse's pass fee, per year of the approval** | {{mm2h-silver:passFeePerYear.dependant:money}} | {{mm2h-gold:passFeePerYear.dependant:money}} |
| **Spouse's visa fee and bond** | By nationality | By nationality |
| **Days in Malaysia required** | None | None |
| **Health insurance** | Not required for either, at application [^3] | Not required for either, at application |
[^1]: {{mm2h-silver:fixedDepositWithdrawable:text}}
[^2]: {{mm2h-silver:propertyStateFloorNote:text}}
[^3]: Both are over 60, the age to which MOTAC's document list applies the insurance requirement (p.23). The renewal list asks for health insurance without that qualifier (p.24), so budget for cover from the first renewal unless told otherwise in writing.

Read the table in three layers. The deposit is still the couple's money, and up to half of it can be drawn back once the purchase completes, for the purchase itself or for medical, education or tourism spending in Malaysia. The property is an asset, but the minimum in the table is unlikely to be the price they pay once the state floor is applied. The fees are the only true cost, and on both tiers they are dominated by the agency fee rather than the participation fee.

For this couple the deciding question is not the fee line. It is whether they want to commit capital for {{mm2h-silver:tenureYears:years}} with a property they must keep for ten, or for {{mm2h-gold:tenureYears:years}} with the property obligation comfortably inside the pass. The five-month stay pattern works on either tier, because neither has a minimum stay at their age.
