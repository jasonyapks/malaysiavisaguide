**Answer first.** You can't file a PVIP or MM2H application yourself, so the agent you pick is the programme's front door. Look the company up on the government's own register, match the registered name and licence number, check the dates, and never pay an individual or a brand name. A website and a WhatsApp number prove nothing.

## You've already had this conversation

You searched for a PVIP or MM2H agent. Three came back. One quoted a figure that was half the other's. One said "we are fully licensed" and sent a brochure. One has a name that sounds like a company you can't find. Nobody explained why the numbers differ, and nobody told you how you'd know if one of them was wrong.

That is the real problem, and it is not your fault. Both programmes make you use an agent, but neither hands you an easy way to tell a licensed one from a lookalike. The check exists. It just sits on three government pages most people never open.

## Why you need an agent at all

For PVIP, Immigration appoints the agencies itself and only they can lodge an application. For MM2H, MOTAC's December 2025 guide says an application should be submitted through an MM2H tour-operating business licensed under the Tourism Industry Act 1992. Its application flowchart routes every step through a registered agent. Older guidance described applying independently, so if an agent tells you it's still possible, ask them to show you where.

Which means the agent decides whether your file is even accepted. It's worth five minutes.

## Checking a PVIP agent

1. **Find the company on Immigration's list.** The Immigration Department publishes the authorised PVIP agencies as a PDF on its eServices site. A searchable copy sits on [the authorised PVIP agent list](/agents/pvip/). A company that is not on it cannot lodge your application.
2. **Check the date on the PDF, not just the name.** The list moves. The edition dated August 2026 carried 74 agencies; the reissue dated 5 October 2026 carries 50. An old screenshot, or a list an agent forwards you, is not proof. Our copy was last checked on 12 October 2026, so open the official PDF yourself on the day you pay.
3. **Ask for the approval letter.** Immigration's PVIP FAQ says each agency holds a written approval valid for three years. Check it is in date and names the company you are about to pay.
4. **Look the company up with SSM.** Immigration's criteria say an appointed agency must be a Sdn. Bhd. with at least RM1,000,000 paid-up capital and at least 51% Malaysian ownership. The SSM company profile shows all three.
5. **Make sure the name on the contract is the name on the approval.** An approval can't be rented out or handed to a middleman. A referrer is not the agency.
6. **Expect a power of attorney.** An agent can act for you only once you've authorised them in writing.

## Checking an MM2H agent

1. **Find the licence on MOTAC's register.** MOTAC publishes a "Licensed MM2H Company" list, and [a searchable copy is here](/agents/mm2h/). For MM2H the licence number matters as much as the name: both must match the register.
2. **Cross-check the second list.** mm2h.gov.my keeps its own list. In our 12 October 2026 comparison, motac.gov.my was the more current of the two, and 12 licences appeared only on mm2h.gov.my, and all but one of those had expired. A company that is on one list and not the other deserves a direct question.
3. **Check the dates.** A licence has a validity period. An agent whose licence has lapsed is not licensed today, whatever it was last year.
4. **Branch offices.** Some companies hold branch licences. Confirm which office you are dealing with and that it matches the licence.
5. **Check the fee against the schedule.** MM2H agency fees are fixed by the government schedule. On Silver the fee is {{mm2h-silver:agencyFee.principal:money}}, on Gold {{mm2h-gold:agencyFee.principal:money}}, and on Platinum {{mm2h-platinum:agencyFee.principal:money}}, all inclusive of 8% SST. A quote materially above those is not an expensive agent. It is a wrong one. We take that schedule from MYPVIP, as at 28 July 2026, because MOTAC's own guide does not print it.

## What the registers can't tell you

A register proves a company is authorised. It does not prove that it is honest, that it's competent, or that the person messaging you works for it. Three gaps are worth closing yourself.

**Is the person real?** Ask for the name of the licensed company and the licence number in writing, then use the phone number and email printed on the register to confirm that the person works there. Don't use the number they gave you.

**Does a licence travel?** Malaysia's tourism minister said on 27 August 2026 that a Malaysian MM2H licence does not satisfy the legal requirements of the countries where agents recruit, and that following local consumer-protection, advertising and immigration rules is on the agent. If you were approached in your own country, ask how the agent is permitted to market there and who your local counterpart is.

**Where does the money go?** The RM1,000,000 PVIP fixed deposit and the MM2H fixed deposit both remain yours, held at a licensed Malaysian bank. You open them. They are not paid to an agent. The PVIP participation fee is staged: only a small part is paid on submission and the balance falls due after approval. If anyone asks for the deposit, or the whole fee, up front, or to a personal account, stop.

## Red flags, in order of seriousness

- **They guarantee approval.** PVIP goes to an evaluation committee and the Director General of Immigration; MM2H is decided by MOTAC. No agent controls either.
- **They won't show the licence number or the approval letter.**
- **The company on the contract is not the company on the register.**
- **Payment to an individual's bank account.**
- **The quote for MM2H is above the government schedule**, or for PVIP is unexplained, with no written statement of what is covered.
- **Pressure to pay before you've seen anything in writing.**

## What to do next

Pick the agent you were going to use. Open the register, find them by registered name, and write down the licence number and the date. If they are not there, you have your answer. If they are, ask for the quote in writing, with what it covers and what comes back if you are refused, and compare it with at least one other authorised agent.

Registers change, agencies are added and removed, and fee schedules get revised. Verify the current position on the official pages on the day you commit; the dates on this page tell you when we last did.
