Most MM2H holders think of renewal as one event at the end of their term. MOTAC's guide describes two. One happens every five years while your tier's term is still running, and it is a fairly routine reissue of the sticker pass. The other happens once the full term is over, and it carries a category fee for every person on the file.

Silver is where the two meet. Its term is {{mm2h-silver:tenureYears:years}}, so a Silver holder reaches the second kind of renewal at the end of year five, at the same point a Gold or Platinum holder is only renewing their sticker. That is why "Silver renewal after five years" is the question people search for, and the answer is yes: it renews, and it keeps renewing.

## The two renewals in MOTAC's guide

Section 13 of MOTAC's Guide: Malaysia My Second Home (December 2025) splits renewal in two.

- **Before the term is complete.** The sticker pass is renewable every five years, or according to the validity of your passport. The fees are a fixed pass fee of {{mm2h-silver:passFeePerYear.dependant:money}} per year and a visa fee set by your nationality.
- **After the term is complete.** The pass can be renewed for every five years after the maximum years of the programme have been completed. You produce a copy of a valid passport, a copy of the latest medical report and health insurance, and you pay a category renewal fee for the principal and for each dependant. The application goes to the One Stop Centre MM2H or the Immigration Department.

Section 12 adds the condition that ties both to your passport: the pass's validity depends on the passport's, and if the passport expires before the pass does, the pass has to be renewed into the new passport.

## When each renewal falls, by tier

{% table caption="When MM2H renewal falls on each tier, and the category fee after the full term" %}
|  | Term | Renewals within the term | Renewal after the term |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Silver** | {{mm2h-silver:tenureYears:years}} | None | RM1,500 per person, every five years [^1] |
| **Gold** | {{mm2h-gold:tenureYears:years}} | Every five years, or with passport validity | RM3,000 per person, every five years [^1] |
| **Platinum** | {{mm2h-platinum:tenureYears:years}} | Every five years, or with passport validity | RM5,000 per person, every five years [^1] |
| **SEZ / SFZ** | 10 years [^1] | At year five [^2] | RM300 per person, every five years [^1] |
[^1]: MOTAC, Guide: Malaysia My Second Home (December 2025), section 13 and the renewal terms on each category page. "Per person" is the principal and each dependant.
[^2]: The ten-year zone-route pass is issued as five years plus five. ((MYPVIP practice, as at 3 August 2026))

Two things follow from the table. A Gold holder does not pay the RM3,000 category fee at year five or year ten; it first falls due at year fifteen. And the category fee is charged per person, so a family of four renewing Platinum after its term pays it four times. Budget for the renewal you will actually face, not the one in a headline figure.

The government-set agency fee you paid at the start covered the main applicant's first five years of pass fee and visa fee. Nothing in that schedule covers the years after them, so every renewal, of either kind, is a new cost. (({{mm2h-silver:agencyFee.attributionBy:text}}, as at {{mm2h-silver:agencyFee.attributionAsAt:date}}))

## What the renewal costs in practice

MOTAC's guide lists the within-term fees and the after-term fee in separate tables, which reads as though the second replaces the first. In practice it does not. A renewal after the full term is charged the pass fee for the new five years, the visa fee for your nationality, and the category renewal fee on top. ((MYPVIP practice, as at July 2026))

So a Silver principal renewing at year five should expect three lines on the bill: five years of the pass fee at {{mm2h-silver:passFeePerYear.dependant:money}} a year, five years of the visa fee at their nationality's rate, and the RM1,500 category fee. Each dependant adds their own pass fee, visa fee and category fee. The [MM2H guide](/visas/mm2h/) lists the agency fee schedule and the per-dependant charges in full.

## What must still be true when you renew

Renewal is not only paperwork. The conditions you entered on still apply, and the file is the point at which some of them are checked.

- **The fixed deposit is still in place.** The renewal file needs the latest balance of the deposit. ((MYPVIP practice, as at July 2026)) The guide allows up to half of the principal to be withdrawn for purchasing a residence, education, medical and tourism activities in Malaysia; what it does not do is let the deposit lapse. When the withdrawal window opens is a separate subject, covered in [the MM2H guide](/visas/mm2h/).
- **You still own the property.** MOTAC's guide says the residence may not be sold for ten years unless you are upgrading to one of higher value. On Silver that obligation runs past the first five-year pass, so it is very much live at the first renewal. A sale before ten years needs immigration's authorisation in advance, and a sale without it puts the pass at risk. ((MYPVIP practice, as at 3 August 2026))
- **You have met the stay requirement.** {{mm2h-silver:minStayPerYear:text}} The guide does not say how this is tested at renewal. What the file does ask for is evidence of your most recent entry, which is the next trap.
- **Your passport covers the new pass.** The pass cannot outlive the passport it sits in. If your passport is close to expiry, renew it first, or you will be transferring the pass into it shortly after paying to renew it.

## How the renewal runs, and how long it takes

1. **Be in Malaysia when you submit.** The file needs a copy of your latest Malaysian entry stamp, so you are physically present to submit it. ((MYPVIP practice, as at July 2026))
2. **Do the medical at a Malaysian clinic on Form MF II.** The form is valid for three months, so leave it undated until you are ready to submit, or it can expire while the rest of the file is still being assembled. ((MYPVIP practice, as at July 2026))
3. **Allow 30 working days for the approval letter.** That is the One Stop Centre's minimum, not a typical figure. ((MYPVIP practice, as at July 2026))
4. **Endorse within three months of the letter.** The endorsement itself takes four to five working days in Malaysia, so plan a trip around it rather than a single visit. ((MYPVIP practice, as at July 2026))

Add those together and the renewal needs roughly two months of lead time before the expiry date at the very least, and two trips if you do not live in Malaysia for most of the year. Count back from your expiry date before you book anything.

## The traps

{% callout tone="warning" title="An autogate entry leaves no stamp" %}
If you entered Malaysia through the biometric autogate, there is no entry stamp in your passport, and the renewal file asks for a copy of one. This was an open question with Immigration as at July 2026. ((MYPVIP practice, as at July 2026)) If a renewal is coming up, enter through a staffed counter on your next arrival and ask for the stamp.
{% endcallout %}

**Dating the medical form early.** Form MF II lapses after three months. A form signed at the start of a slow file can be out of date by the time the file is submitted.

**Treating the first Silver renewal as a release.** The five-year pass ends; the ten-year property hold does not. A Silver holder who sells at year five to fund something else has broken a condition of the pass they are trying to renew.

**Budgeting a Gold or Platinum renewal at the wrong figure.** The within-term renewals are pass and visa fees only. The category fee arrives at the end of the full term, and it multiplies by the number of people on the file.

**Letting the passport decide the timing.** Because the pass follows passport validity, a passport expiring mid-term forces a renewal of the sticker into the new passport. That can be planned. Left to the last month, it collides with travel.

**Assuming age changes nothing.** The stay requirement depends on the principal's age, not the tier, and it falls away from 50. Other conditions do not. The deposit, the property and the documents are the same at 60 as at 40.

> The pass you renew is new. The conditions you renew it under are the ones you signed up to.

## Before you start the renewal

Check three things at least three months before expiry: that your passport will outlast the new pass, that your fixed deposit is still in place at the balance the programme expects, and that the property you bought is still in your name. If all three hold, the rest is timing. If one of them does not, deal with that first, because no amount of paperwork will carry a renewal past it.
