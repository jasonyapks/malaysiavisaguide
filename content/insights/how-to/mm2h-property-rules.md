You picked Silver because the brochure said you could be in from {{mm2h-silver:propertyPurchaseMin:money}}. Then you started looking at condos in Kuala Lumpur, and a lawyer told you that you can't buy anything under RM1,000,000. In Selangor it was RM2,000,000.

You haven't misread anything. The RM600,000 figure is real. It just isn't the number that decides what you can buy.

That's the first of several things about the MM2H property rule that tend to surface late, usually after you've chosen a tier. This page puts them in front of you before you choose.

**The short answer:** every MM2H tier makes you buy a residential property in Malaysia. You must complete the purchase within a year of your visa being endorsed and keep it for ten years. The price you'll actually pay starts at whichever is higher: your tier's minimum or the floor set by the state you buy in.

## What MM2H makes you buy

The rule itself is short. The consequences take longer to explain.

- **It's compulsory on every tier.** Silver, Gold and Platinum all require it. Renting doesn't count.
- **It must be residential.** A shop lot or an office doesn't qualify.
- **It must meet your tier's minimum.** That's {{mm2h-silver:propertyPurchaseMin:money}} on Silver, {{mm2h-gold:propertyPurchaseMin:money}} on Gold and {{mm2h-platinum:propertyPurchaseMin:money}} on Platinum.
- **The clock starts at endorsement.** You have one year from the day your visa goes into your passport to complete the purchase. ((MYPVIP practice, as at 3 August 2026))
- **You hold it for ten years.** You can sell earlier only to trade up to a property of higher value, and only with immigration's authorisation in advance. ((MYPVIP practice, as at 3 August 2026))

That last rule changes what kind of decision this is. You're not renting a flat while you try out a country. You're making a ten-year commitment to one property, in one place, at a price that suits the state rather than you.

## The state floor: the number that actually binds

MOTAC's own fee schedule says minimum purchase prices are "also subject to respective State Laws". Land in Malaysia is a state matter, and each state sets its own minimum price for foreign buyers. Where that floor is higher than your tier's minimum, the state's floor applies.

In the two places most MM2H applicants buy, the gap is large.

| Where you buy | State floor for foreign buyers | What it means for Silver |
|---|---|---|
| Kuala Lumpur | RM1,000,000 | You pay at least RM400,000 more than the brochure figure |
| Selangor | RM2,000,000 | You pay at least RM1,400,000 more, and more than Platinum's minimum |

Other states, including Penang and Johor, set different floors for different areas and property types. The published figures don't always agree with each other, so treat any number you find online as a starting point. **Get the floor for the exact property confirmed in writing by a conveyancing lawyer before you choose your tier.** If the home you want costs more than RM1,000,000 anyway, Gold's higher property minimum may cost you nothing extra.

## What foreigners can't buy

Even above the floor, not every residential property is open to you. As a general rule, a foreign buyer can't buy:

- land on Malay Reserve, or units set aside for Bumiputera buyers, and
- low-cost and low-to-medium-cost housing.

Every foreign purchase also needs the consent of the state authority. That consent takes time, and it runs inside your one-year window. Ask your lawyer how long consent is currently taking in the state you're buying in, and build that into your timeline.

## What it costs on top of the price

The purchase price is the headline. These costs sit on top of it.

- **Stamp duty on the transfer.** Budget 2026 set a flat **8%** on the transfer of residential property to a foreign buyer, from 1 January 2026, up from 4%. On a RM1,000,000 condo, that's RM80,000. Confirm the rate with your lawyer before you sign.
- **Stamp duty on a loan,** if you borrow, charged separately on the loan agreement.
- **Legal fees** for the Sale and Purchase Agreement and the transfer.
- **State consent fees,** which vary by state.
- **Running costs:** maintenance and sinking fund charges, quit rent, assessment tax and insurance, every year for as long as you own it.
- **Tax when you sell.** As a foreigner, you pay Real Property Gains Tax of 30% on the gain if you sell within five years, and 10% after that. Unlike citizens, you never reach 0%. Our [guide to tax for expats](/insights/expat-living/malaysian-tax-for-expats/) has the detail.

## Paying for it with the fixed deposit

You don't have to fund the whole purchase from outside the programme. {{mm2h-silver:fixedDepositWithdrawable:text}} ((MYPVIP practice, as at 17 October 2026))

There's a catch in the order. You need the signed agreement to make the withdrawal. So the booking fee and the deposit you pay on signing still come from your own money. Plan your cash for that first stage. [Our fixed deposit guide](/insights/how-to/mm2h-fixed-deposit-withdrawal/) covers what has to stay in the account.

## Which buyer are you?

**You'd buy a home in Malaysia anyway.** Then the property rule costs you very little. Choose the area first, find out its state floor, and let that number help you choose the tier. If the floor is RM1,000,000 or more, look hard at Gold.

**You want Kuala Lumpur or Selangor on a Silver budget.** Do the arithmetic before you apply. In Selangor, the state floor is already above Platinum's minimum. Silver may still be the right tier, but the property won't be the cheap part.

**You'd rather rent for a few years first.** Federal MM2H won't let you. Rent on a visitor stay before you apply, or look at a programme without a purchase rule. [Federal MM2H vs Sarawak MM2H](/insights/comparisons/federal-mm2h-vs-sarawak-mm2h/) compares the main alternative.

**You might need to leave within ten years.** Think carefully. You can trade up, but you can't simply sell and walk away inside the ten years without putting your pass at risk. If your plans are genuinely uncertain, the property rule is the strongest argument against federal MM2H.
