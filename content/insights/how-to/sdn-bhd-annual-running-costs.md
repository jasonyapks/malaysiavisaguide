You budgeted for the incorporation. Then the invoices started: a retainer every quarter, a filing fee you'd never heard of, an accountant, an auditor, a tax agent. None of them is large. Together they come to more than the company cost to open, and they come every year.

Here's what you've probably noticed and not seen admitted: a Malaysian company costs about the same to keep whether it earns a million ringgit or nothing at all. The fixed costs are for existing, not for trading.

**The short answer:** a foreign-owned Sdn Bhd costs roughly RM4,000 to RM12,000 a year to keep compliant if it's small, and RM10,000 to RM20,000 once it has staff. Add about RM7,200 a year if you live abroad and need a nominee director. The lines are a company secretary, annual filings, accounts, an audit unless exempt, and a tax return, plus 8% service tax on the fees.

## What every company pays, trading or not

All figures are in ringgit before 8% service tax, and are indicative ranges based on a published price list and a written quote from two Malaysian corporate service firms, reviewed in October 2026.

| Yearly cost | Typical range | Why you can't skip it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Company secretary retainer** | 1,200–1,450 | Every company must have one. Usually includes the registered office. |
| **Annual return** | 300–500, plus a RM150 SSM fee | Due within 30 days of each incorporation anniversary. |
| **Beneficial ownership filing** | About 50 per shareholder | The company must record who ultimately owns or controls it. |
| **Lodging financial statements** | 200–600, plus SSM fees | Required every year, audited or not. |
| **Bookkeeping and accounts** | 1,000 a year to 550 a month | Someone has to produce the numbers. |
| **Audit** | 1,000–2,500, or nil if exempt | See below. |
| **Company tax return** | 700–1,200 | Filed every year, even with no income. |

Some firms fold the annual return and lodgements into a higher monthly retainer. Others quote a low retainer and bill each filing. When you compare two quotes, add up the year, not the month.

## Which company is yours?

**Dormant, or barely used.** A holding company, or one you set up and haven't started. Secretary, annual return, a simple set of accounts and a tax return: about RM4,000 to RM5,000 a year. This is the company people forget they're paying for.

**Small and trading.** A consultancy or small services business with a handful of invoices a month. The same fixed lines, with bookkeeping and audit as the swing factors: about RM4,000 at the lean end to RM12,000 with monthly bookkeeping and a full audit.

**Employing people.** Once you hire, add payroll. Registering with the provident fund, social security and the tax office is typically a one-off of about RM600. Outsourced payroll runs from about RM200 a month for a couple of staff, rising with headcount. Expect RM10,000 to RM20,000 a year all in.

## The four lines that surprise foreign owners

**The nominee director doesn't end.** If you live abroad, the resident director you appointed at incorporation is an annual cost, typically about RM600 a month. It stops only when someone who lives in Malaysia, possibly you, takes the seat.

**You probably don't get the small-company tax rate.** Malaysian small companies pay 15% on their first RM150,000 of chargeable income and 17% on the next RM450,000. A company that is more than 20% foreign-owned is shut out and pays 24% from the first ringgit. On RM300,000 of profit that's the difference between about RM48,000 and RM72,000 in tax. The test is explained in [Malaysian tax for expats](/insights/expat-living/malaysian-tax-for-expats/).

**Audit exemption is real, and it still costs something.** A private company can now skip the statutory audit if it meets two of three size tests on revenue, total assets and number of employees. The revenue test is RM2,000,000 for financial years starting in 2026 and RM3,000,000 from 2027. If you qualify, you save the audit fee, but you'll still pay a few hundred ringgit for the exemption paperwork and you still lodge unaudited accounts. Ask your secretary to confirm you qualify before you assume it.

**Your foreign staff cost more than their salary.** Employers must now make provident fund contributions for non-citizen employees as well as Malaysians. If your company employs you on an Employment Pass, that includes you.

## Changes cost extra

A basic retainer covers standing still. Anything that changes the company is charged separately, typically RM250 to RM750 each:

- Appointing or removing a director
- Transferring or issuing shares, or increasing paid-up capital
- Changing the registered address, the company name or the nature of the business
- Declaring a dividend
- Opening another bank account

A founder who replaces a nominee, injects capital for an Employment Pass and pays a first dividend will use three of those in a year. Some firms sell a higher tier that includes a set number of changes. It's worth it in a busy first year and not after.

## What it costs to stop

This is the part to read before you incorporate, not after.

You can't abandon a Malaysian company. Until it's formally closed, the filings keep falling due, and missed filings become penalties against the company and its directors.

**Striking off** is the cheap exit, for a company that has stopped trading and has no assets or debts. Professional fees are typically about RM1,500 to RM2,500, and it takes months.

**Members' voluntary winding up** is the proper exit where there's money or property to return to shareholders. Expect about RM12,000 to RM18,000 and a liquidator.

If you put RM500,000 of paid-up capital into the company for an Employment Pass and later want it back, this is how it comes out. Budget for the exit when you budget for the entrance.

## So what should you plan for?

If the company is a **shell you might use**, ask whether you need it yet. RM4,000 to RM5,000 a year for an option you never exercise adds up.

If it's a **small working business**, budget RM8,000 a year and you'll rarely be surprised. Get the audit-exemption question answered in year one.

If it **employs people, including you**, budget RM15,000, and set aside the tax at 24%, not at the rates in the brochure.

And if you're paying for a nominee director only because you don't live here yet, the cheapest saving on this whole page is becoming a resident yourself.
