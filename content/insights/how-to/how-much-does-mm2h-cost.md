You've asked three people what MM2H costs and got three answers. A forum says the fee is {{mm2h-silver:participationFee.principal:money}}. An agent quotes {{mm2h-silver:agencyFee.principal:money}}. A relocation blog says you need a million ringgit. All three are right about something, and none of them is telling you the whole bill.

Most MM2H quotes are confusing because they mix three kinds of money. Some of it you pay and never see again. Some of it you park in a bank and keep. Some of it buys you a house. Fold them into one number and the number means nothing.

**The short answer:** for one main applicant, MM2H's fees come to about RM41,000 on Silver, RM58,000 on Gold and RM270,000 on Platinum. On top of the fees you place a fixed deposit of {{mm2h-silver:fixedDeposit:money}}, {{mm2h-gold:fixedDeposit:money}} or {{mm2h-platinum:fixedDeposit:money}}, and you buy a property in Malaysia. The deposit and the property stay yours. The fees don't.

## The fees: what you pay and don't get back

There are only two fees on the main applicant, and both are fixed.

| | Silver | Gold | Platinum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Agency fee** | {{mm2h-silver:agencyFee.principal:money}} [^1] | {{mm2h-gold:agencyFee.principal:money}} | {{mm2h-platinum:agencyFee.principal:money}} |
| **Participation fee** | {{mm2h-silver:participationFee.principal:money}} [^2] | {{mm2h-gold:participationFee.principal:money}} | {{mm2h-platinum:participationFee.principal:money}} |
| **Fees, main applicant** | **About RM41,000** | **About RM58,000** | **About RM270,000** |
| **Term** | {{mm2h-silver:tenureYears:years}} | {{mm2h-gold:tenureYears:years}} | {{mm2h-platinum:tenureYears:years}} |
[^1]: Fixed by the government and inclusive of 8% SST. It covers the main applicant's processing fee, their first five years of pass fee and visa fee, and their security bond. {{mm2h-silver:agencyFee.attributionBy:text}}, as at {{mm2h-silver:agencyFee.attributionAsAt:date}}.
[^2]: Charged per application, not per person, so adding a spouse or children does not multiply it.

Here's the fact that saves people money: **on MM2H, the agency fee is not the agency's to set.** The government fixes it. There's nothing to shop around for, and nothing to negotiate. A quote above these figures is wrong rather than expensive. If someone offers you a "discount", ask what they've left out.

The payment terms are fixed as well: {{mm2h-silver:agencyFee.paymentTerms:text}} Before you pay the first 20%, ask in writing what happens to it if the application is refused. That's the question people forget to ask.

## Dependants: the line most quotes leave out

The headline fee is for one person. Bring your spouse, children or parents and each of them adds their own charges:

- **Processing fee:** {{mm2h-silver:processingFee.dependant:money}} per dependant, paid to the government.
- **Pass fee:** {{mm2h-silver:passFeePerYear.dependant:money}} per dependant for each year of the pass.
- **Visa fee:** per dependant per year, set by their nationality. For most passports it's a small sum, but it varies.
- **Security bond:** a nominal RM10 per dependant.
- **Extra agency fee:** the main fee already covers your first dependant. From the second one onwards it's {{mm2h-silver:agencyFee.perDependant:money}} each.

So a retired couple on Silver, five years, pays about RM46,000 in fees, not RM41,000. A family of four pays noticeably more again. The [cost calculator](/tools/cost-calculator/) adds it up for your passport and your family size, line by line.

## The capital: what you park and what you buy

This is where the real money is, and where most budgets go wrong.

**The fixed deposit.** {{mm2h-silver:fixedDeposit:money}} on Silver, {{mm2h-gold:fixedDeposit:money}} on Gold, {{mm2h-platinum:fixedDeposit:money}} on Platinum, placed in a Malaysian bank in your name. It earns interest and it's still your money. {{mm2h-silver:fixedDepositWithdrawable:text}} The bank may ask you to convert it to ringgit at the market rate, so currency movement is a real cost if your life is priced in dollars or pounds. The timing is MYPVIP practice, as at 17 October 2026; the permitted uses are in MOTAC's guide.

**The property.** Compulsory on all three tiers. The programme minimum is {{mm2h-silver:propertyPurchaseMin:money}} on Silver, {{mm2h-gold:propertyPurchaseMin:money}} on Gold and {{mm2h-platinum:propertyPurchaseMin:money}} on Platinum. {{mm2h-silver:propertyStateFloorNote:text}}

Two conditions come with it. The purchase has to complete within the first year after your visa is endorsed, and you have to hold the property for ten years. Selling earlier needs immigration's approval, and only to buy something of higher value. On Silver, that ten years outlasts your first five-year pass. Both conditions are stated by MYPVIP practice, as at 3 August 2026.

Here's the trap: the money has to be there twice. The deposit goes in first, in full. Half of it can come back once your visa is endorsed and you've signed the Sale and Purchase Agreement, and that can help with the balance of the price. But the booking fee and the payment on signing come out of your own pocket. **Budget the full deposit plus the opening payments on the property as cash you need at the same time.**

## The costs nobody puts on the quote

None of these is large, but together they're real:

- **The trip to Malaysia.** After approval you have 90 days to come in, place the deposit, do the medical and collect the visa. Allow about five working days in Kuala Lumpur, and book three weeks ahead for the endorsement appointment.
- **The medical.** A consultation at a Malaysian clinic, usually under RM100 per person.
- **Medical insurance.** Required for each person and priced by the insurer, not the government. Over-60s are exempt.
- **Documents from home.** A police clearance certificate, certified copies and sometimes translation or legalisation. The police certificate is usually the slowest part.

The timings, the medical figure and the insurance exemption above are MYPVIP practice, as at July 2026.
- **Renewal.** Not a cost today, but it is coming. See [MM2H renewal requirements and fees](/insights/how-to/mm2h-renewal-requirements-and-fees/).

## Which bill is yours?

**You're retiring and want somewhere warm, affordable and easy.** Silver. About RM41,000 in fees for you, a few thousand more for your spouse, and the property is the big decision. Choose the state before you choose the house, because the state floor may decide it for you. Silver doesn't let you work or run a business here.

**You want a longer horizon and you're bringing family.** Gold buys {{mm2h-gold:tenureYears:years}} instead of {{mm2h-silver:tenureYears:years}}, for about RM17,000 more in fees. The real step up is the deposit, which more than triples. If you'll still be here in year six, compare what renewing Silver would cost you then.

**You want to work, invest or run a company here.** Platinum is the only tier that allows it, and its participation fee alone is {{mm2h-platinum:participationFee.principal:money}}. Before you pay that, read [MM2H Platinum vs PVIP](/insights/comparisons/mm2h-platinum-vs-pvip/) and [which Malaysian visa lets you run a business](/insights/comparisons/which-visa-lets-you-run-a-business/). For some people a different route does the same job for less.

**You're not sure MM2H is the right programme at all.** Then the fees are the wrong place to start. [MM2H vs PVIP vs DE Rantau](/insights/comparisons/mm2h-vs-pvip-vs-de-rantau/) compares what each one asks of you, and Sarawak runs its own version on different terms: see [Federal MM2H vs Sarawak MM2H](/insights/comparisons/federal-mm2h-vs-sarawak-mm2h/).

Once you know your tier, [how to apply for MM2H in 2026](/insights/how-to/apply-for-mm2h-2026/) takes you through the steps in order.
