The MM2H fixed deposit is usually described as money you park and forget. It is closer to money you park, partly get back, and then cannot touch again for as long as you hold the pass. Half of it is a condition of the visa. The other half can come back out, but only for four kinds of spending, and later than most applicants assume.

This piece covers what the rules say about that withdrawal, where MOTAC's guide contradicts itself, and what is applied in practice. It also covers the part that is genuinely unanswered, because an honest gap is more useful than a confident guess.

## How much of the MM2H fixed deposit can be withdrawn?

Up to half of the principal. MOTAC's guide caps it at "a maximum withdrawal of 50%" of the principal fixed deposit value, and the clause is the same on every category page (Silver p.37, Gold p.33). Two details in that wording matter. The ceiling is set against the **principal**, the amount you placed, not against whatever the account holds after interest. And it is a ceiling, not an entitlement to a lump sum: nothing in the guide requires you to draw it at all.

The deposit is placed in US dollars on the published figures, with a Malaysian bank. The bank may require it to be converted into ringgit at the market rate. ((MYPVIP practice, as at March 2026)) If that happens, both the half you can withdraw and the half that stays in carry exchange-rate exposure for the life of the pass, which is worth weighing if your pension or savings are in another currency.

## What can the MM2H deposit withdrawal be used for?

Four purposes, and they are published rather than a matter of practice. Each category page of MOTAC's guide allows the withdrawal "for the purposes of purchasing a residence, education, medical and tourism activities in Malaysia" (Silver p.37).

- **Purchasing a residence.** The most common use, and the one with a timing catch, covered below.
- **Education.** For example, fees for a dependant child studying in Malaysia. The guide allows dependant children to study up to tertiary level at a government-recognised institution (p.20).
- **Medical.** The guide also allows long-term medical treatment in Malaysia within the pass's validity (p.20), which makes this the use that matters most to older participants.
- **Tourism activities.** Travel and leisure spending inside the country.

Every item on the list ends with "in Malaysia". Spending abroad, investing, or moving the money back home is not on it.

## When does the withdrawal window open?

This is where the sources disagree, and there are three versions.

1. **MOTAC's category pages** say the withdrawal is allowed "after the approval as MM2H participant has been obtained" (Silver p.37, Gold p.33).
2. **MOTAC's summary page** says the withdrawal is allowed "starting from the second year onwards after the approval" (p.18). The same document therefore gives two different start points.
3. **In practice**, the window opens once the property purchase completes, with no further waiting period. On Silver, Gold and Platinum the purchase follows approval, so the order is approval, then purchase, then withdrawal. ((MYPVIP practice, as at 23 August 2026))

Only the timing departs from the guide. The permitted uses, the purchase of a residence among them, are in the guide verbatim.

## Why the deposit cannot pay for your property

Buying a residence is a permitted use, and yet the deposit cannot be your down payment. The reason is the order of events. Because the withdrawal opens when the purchase completes, the money comes back after you have paid, so it reimburses the purchase rather than financing it. ((MYPVIP practice, as at 23 August 2026))

That has one practical consequence that most budgets miss: **you need the full deposit and the full purchase price available at the same time.** On Silver, that means placing {{mm2h-silver:fixedDeposit:money}} and then completing a purchase of at least {{mm2h-silver:propertyPurchaseMin:money}}, before any of the deposit comes back. {{mm2h-silver:propertyStateFloorNote:text}}

The clock on the purchase is also short. It must complete within one year of the pass being endorsed, and the property must then be held for ten years unless you are trading up to one of higher value. ((MYPVIP practice, as at 3 August 2026)) The holding period is also in MOTAC's guide (p.20). So the earliest realistic withdrawal is some months into the pass, and it is tied to a property you cannot easily sell.

## What must stay in the MM2H fixed deposit?

At least half of the principal, for the life of the pass. The guide states no exception to the 50% ceiling, and each category page ends with the same sanction: failure to comply with any of the terms results in the pass being revoked (Silver p.37).

The balance is checked again later. In practice, a renewal file includes the latest fixed deposit balance. ((MYPVIP practice, as at July 2026)) A participant who has drawn the deposit below the permitted level should expect that to surface at renewal, if not before.

The guide also lists the deposit among the category benefits: participants receive a tax exemption on foreign funds or income such as their fixed deposit (p.20). That is a point about tax on incoming money, not a reason to treat the account as freely accessible.

## What happens to the deposit at the end of the term, or on cancellation?

Neither MOTAC's guide nor any practice we can attribute says what happens to the remaining deposit when the pass ends, whether because the term runs out without renewal, because the pass is cancelled or revoked, or because the participant gives it up. We are not going to fill that gap with an assumption.

Here is what is published around it.

- **The pass can continue past its term.** The guide provides for renewal every five years after the maximum years of the programme have been completed (p.24). Its list of renewal documents is a passport, a medical report and health insurance, and it does not mention the deposit.
- **A breach ends the pass.** Failure to comply with any term results in revocation (Silver p.37). The guide does not describe what follows for the deposit.
- **The pass can pass to a spouse.** On the death of the principal, the pass is transferable to the next of kin among the registered dependants (Silver p.36). The guide does not say how the deposit is treated in that case.

The practical step is to ask before you place the money, not after. Ask the bank what it needs in order to release the balance when the pass ends, and ask your licensed agent what the One Stop Centre requires. Get both answers in writing and keep them with the deposit certificate.

## SEZ and SFZ: same uses, unsettled timing

The SEZ page of MOTAC's guide carries the same permitted-use clause as the three main tiers (p.43). The timing is a different question. On the zone routes, the purchase inside the designated development is a condition of approval rather than a step after it. "After approval" and "on completion of the purchase" are therefore different events there, and we have not been able to establish which one opens the window. Until we can, we are not stating either.
