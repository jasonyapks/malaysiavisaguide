Ask anyone where foreigners live in Kuala Lumpur and you'll get the same answer: Mont Kiara. Ask why, and the answer is usually "that's where the other foreigners are." That's a reason. It's just not always yours.

Mont Kiara exists, as a place to live, because of two international schools and the condos that grew up around them. If your children go to one of those schools, living there saves you an hour a day in traffic, and that's worth a lot. If they don't, or you don't have children, you may be paying for a postcode built around somebody else's school run.

**The short answer:** as at October 2026, a two-bedroom condo lists for about RM2,800 to RM3,200 a month in Mont Kiara, RM3,200 to RM4,800 in Bangsar, RM3,400 to RM4,200 in the KLCC area and about RM2,100 in Cheras. The right area depends less on the rent than on where your days happen: a school, an office, or nowhere in particular.

Where an area has few buildings in the sample, a single development can dominate it, so read the numbers as indicative. Three things jump out.

**Size matters more than area in some places.** In Ampang Hilir, the median three-bedroom listing was RM12,000 a month. That's the embassy belt, where three-bedroom homes are large and often serviced, and it sits next to ordinary two-bedrooms at RM3,000. In Bukit Jalil three-bedrooms were *cheaper* than two-bedrooms in our sample, because the three-bedroom stock there is mostly older, larger blocks.

**Bangsar costs more than Mont Kiara for one and two bedrooms.** That surprises people, because Mont Kiara has the reputation. Bangsar's premium is for an older, established neighbourhood with shops and restaurants you can walk to, rather than a cluster of tower blocks.

**Agreed rents are lower.** These are asking prices. Landlords with empty units negotiate, especially on a longer lease or when you'll pay several months up front. Treat the median as a ceiling for a first offer, not a floor.

## Which area is yours?

**Your children go to Garden International School or Mont'Kiara International School.** Both are in Mont Kiara, so live in Mont Kiara or Sri Hartamas next door. The rent premium is smaller than the cost of a daily school run across the city, in money and in time. A three-bedroom family unit runs about RM3,500 to RM5,800.

**Your children go to ISKL.** The school is in Ampang Hilir, on the other side of the city from Mont Kiara. Look at Ampang Hilir itself or the KLCC area, which is a short drive away. Be ready for Ampang Hilir's large family homes to be priced at embassy rates.

**You're retired and want space and green rather than nightlife.** Desa ParkCity is a planned township around a large park, built for families and walkers, and its rents show it. A three-bedroom has a median of RM7,000, the highest of any area here apart from Ampang Hilir. If that's more than you want to spend, the same kind of quiet is available at a lower price further out.

**You want a city life without a car.** The KLCC area puts you near the Petronas Towers, the park, the malls and the train lines. Two-bedroom units mostly list at RM3,400 to RM4,200, and you'll save on a car, which matters more for a foreigner than for a local, because you pay the unsubsidised petrol price.

**You want restaurants, cafés and a neighbourhood that feels lived in.** Bangsar. You'll pay for it: RM3,200 to RM4,800 for two bedrooms.

**You work in a modern office and want a short commute.** Bangsar South is a business district with newer residential towers and the most consistent pricing in the sample. Two-bedrooms list at RM3,400 to RM3,700.

**You want the most space for the money.** Cheras and Bukit Jalil. Two-bedrooms list at around RM2,100 in Cheras, and three-bedrooms at around RM2,400 in Bukit Jalil. You'll live among Malaysian families rather than other expats, with a longer drive to the international schools. For some people that's the point.

## The cash you need before you move in

The rent is only the start. Kuala Lumpur landlords usually ask for:

- **Two months' rent as a security deposit,** refundable at the end of the lease, less any proven damage.
- **Half a month's rent as a utility deposit.**
- **The first month's rent in advance.**

That's **three and a half months' rent in cash** before you get the keys: about RM10,500 on a RM3,000 two-bedroom. None of this is set by law. Malaysia has no residential tenancy act in force, so the tenancy agreement decides, and a landlord can ask for more. Read the deposit refund clause before you sign, because that's where most disputes start.

You'll also pay **stamp duty on the tenancy agreement**, usually as the tenant. The agreement has to be stamped with the Inland Revenue Board to be enforceable. Ask the agent to show you the stamped copy.

What you won't pay is **service tax on your rent**. When service tax was extended to rental in July 2025, residential property was left out. The Customs Department's FAQ confirms it with a worked example. If a landlord or agent adds "SST" to a residential rent, ask them to show you where it comes from.

## Rent or buy?

As a foreigner you can buy, but not cheaply. **Kuala Lumpur's minimum price for a foreign buyer is RM1,000,000**, and Selangor's, which covers areas such as Petaling Jaya and Subang, is RM2,000,000. At the rents above, RM1,000,000 buys you the equivalent of almost 28 years of a Mont Kiara two-bedroom, so for most people on a fixed-term pass, renting is the cheaper choice.

The exception is MM2H, where buying is compulsory. Silver requires a purchase of at least {{mm2h-silver:propertyPurchaseMin:money}}, and in Kuala Lumpur the RM1,000,000 state floor applies instead. If you're going to own anyway, choose the neighbourhood the way you'd choose it as a tenant, then buy there. [How much MM2H costs](/insights/how-to/how-much-does-mm2h-cost/) explains how the property rule works.
