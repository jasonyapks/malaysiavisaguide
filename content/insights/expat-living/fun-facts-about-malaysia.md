Malaysia is easy to arrive in and slow to figure out. Here are 24 things that surprise most newcomers, from a king who changes every five years to the fruit you're not allowed to take on the train.

## A country that does things its own way

**1. The king is elected.** Malaysia has nine hereditary Malay rulers, and every five years they elect one of their own as king, the Yang di-Pertuan Agong. The current king is Sultan Ibrahim of Johor, whose term began on 31 January 2024. No other country chooses its monarch this way.

**2. There are two capitals, sort of.** Kuala Lumpur is the official capital, but the federal government moved most of its ministries to Putrajaya, a planned city about 25 km south, from 1999. If your visa paperwork sends you to a ministry, check which city it's in first.

{% figure asset="f594f9f4-1ac2-4129-8571-46e59806fac9" caption="Perdana Putra in Putrajaya, the prime minister's office and the seat of the federal government." %}

**3. The weekend depends on which state you're in.** Kedah, Kelantan and Terengganu take Friday and Saturday off. Johor did the same from 2014, then switched back to Saturday and Sunday on 1 January 2025. Everywhere else rests on Saturday and Sunday. It matters if you're booking a government appointment or a delivery across state lines.

**4. A domestic flight can mean an immigration check.** Sabah and Sarawak, the two Malaysian states on Borneo, kept control of their own immigration when they joined Malaysia in 1963. Fly from KL to Kuching or Kota Kinabalu and you'll queue at an immigration counter on arrival. Foreigners need their passport.

{% figure asset="a6bbcf8d-3996-46c1-8151-e4faf5a4dd8e" caption="The Kuching Waterfront in Sarawak. Fly here from KL and you clear immigration on arrival." %}

**5. Public holidays change from state to state too.** On top of the national holidays, each state has its own, including the birthday of its ruler. Your colleague in Penang might have a day off that you don't have in KL.

**6. Malaysia is older than Malaysia.** Malaya became independent on 31 August 1957, which is Merdeka Day. Malaysia itself was formed on 16 September 1963, when Sabah, Sarawak and Singapore joined. Singapore left in 1965. Both dates are public holidays.

## The name game

**7. Kuala Lumpur means "muddy confluence".** The city grew up where the Klang and Gombak rivers meet, as a tin-mining settlement. The mosque at the meeting point, Masjid Jamek, is still there.

{% figure asset="4366f0df-1323-4459-8a2d-3f8fd0a6e5b2" caption="Masjid Jamek stands where the Gombak and Klang rivers meet, the \"muddy confluence\" that gave Kuala Lumpur its name." %}

**8. The national flower is the hibiscus.** In Malay it's the bunga raya, and once you know it you'll spot it on logos and souvenirs all over the country.

## Building tall

**9. The Petronas Twin Towers were once the tallest buildings in the world.** At 452 metres, they held the title from 1998 until Taipei 101 overtook them in 2004. The skybridge between them is open to visitors.

{% figure asset="20c12278-ec82-46ae-833d-834793d21868" caption="The Petronas Twin Towers, the world's tallest buildings from 1998 to 2004." %}

**10. KL now has the world's second-tallest building.** Merdeka 118 stands 678.9 metres tall. Only the Burj Khalifa in Dubai is taller.

{% figure asset="48fb289f-7365-41dc-8ec9-1574cae1f598" caption="Merdeka 118 at night. At 678.9 metres it's the second-tallest building in the world." %}

**11. Penang has one of the longest bridges in Southeast Asia.** The Penang Second Bridge, opened in 2014, runs about 24 km from the island to the mainland. Most of it is over water.

{% figure asset="7253f586-35bd-45e7-8e16-a40267d242eb" caption="The Penang Second Bridge runs about 24 km, most of it over water." %}

**12. The Batu Caves steps are a workout.** There are 272 of them, painted in rainbow colours, below a 42.7-metre golden statue of the Hindu deity Murugan. During Thaipusam, hundreds of thousands of devotees make the climb.

{% figure asset="d268697f-1008-41f2-81af-dc2a9a021ffa" caption="The 42.7-metre statue of Murugan and the 272 rainbow steps at Batu Caves." %}

## Wild Malaysia

**13. The Malayan tiger is critically endangered.** The wildlife department believes fewer than 150 remain in the wild. That makes it one of the rarest big cats on the planet, and it's also the animal on the national coat of arms.

{% figure asset="a2ca1945-6668-4aa9-8a97-6ef23528ef60" caption="A Malayan tiger. Fewer than 150 are believed to remain in the wild. (Photographed at Dortmund Zoo.)" %}

**14. Malaysia is one of the world's 17 megadiverse countries.** It's on the short list of countries that together hold most of the planet's species.

**15. You can climb the highest mountain between the Himalayas and New Guinea.** Mount Kinabalu in Sabah is 4,095 metres high, and most people summit it on a two-day hike with no technical climbing.

{% figure asset="074a7e60-9dc6-47f1-8e1c-e47e8891ccf7" caption="Mount Kinabalu in Sabah, 4,095 metres high." %}

**16. One cave chamber in Sarawak could hold several jumbo jets.** The Sarawak Chamber in Gunung Mulu National Park is one of the largest known cave chambers in the world.

{% figure asset="2f7b2cf1-47ee-41b3-88fd-3113f17d12d5" caption="Cave of the Winds, one of the show caves in Gunung Mulu National Park, Sarawak." %}

**17. Borneo's orangutans have their own rehabilitation centre.** The Sepilok Orangutan Rehabilitation Centre in Sabah has been caring for orphaned orangutans since 1964. You can watch the feeding sessions.

{% figure asset="3d65497f-65f3-48a3-80a2-1a422c3def5d" caption="An orangutan heading to a feeding platform at Sepilok, Sabah." %}

**18. Langkawi is not one island.** It's an archipelago of 99 islands off the coast of Kedah, and the whole island group is duty-free.

**19. Malaysia has five UNESCO World Heritage Sites.** They are Kinabalu Park, Gunung Mulu National Park, the historic cities of Melaka and George Town, the Lenggong Valley, and the Niah Caves, the most recent addition, from 2024.

## Food, fruit and the rules around them

**20. Durian is banned on trains.** The smell of the "king of fruits" is strong enough that RapidKL trains and many hotels post no-durian signs. Malaysians treat this as perfectly reasonable.

{% figure asset="bf270556-0a83-4adf-8bce-a150b65361bb" caption="Durians at Air Itam, Penang. The smell is why trains and hotels ban them." %}

**21. Mamak restaurants never close.** Many of these Indian-Muslim eateries open 24 hours a day, which is why you'll find people watching football over teh tarik at 3am.

**22. Breakfast is the big meal for a lot of people.** Nasi lemak, coconut rice with sambal, anchovies, peanuts and egg, is widely called the unofficial national dish, and most people eat it in the morning.

{% figure asset="ab5245d3-a73d-41a0-8e7e-d9c1ad9c3d81" caption="Nasi lemak: coconut rice with sambal, anchovies, peanuts and egg." %}

## Business and history

**23. Malaysia's rubber came from London.** Rubber seeds sent from Kew Gardens reached Malaya in 1877. Within decades, rubber plantations were a pillar of the colonial economy.

**24. Malaysia makes its own cars.** Proton, the national car company, launched its first model, the Proton Saga, in 1985. You'll still see Sagas on every road.

{% figure asset="45bb96d8-dc83-4055-86d3-5dde3f8d1d3a" caption="Three generations of the Proton Saga, Malaysia's first national car." %}

## Living with the quirks

Most of these are trivia. Three of them affect everyday planning: weekends that change across state lines, holidays that vary by state, and an immigration check on flights to Borneo.
