**Answer first.** A British passport already gets you 90 days in Malaysia, visa-free. Everything beyond that is one of four routes: MM2H if you're retiring, PVIP if you're still earning, DE Rantau if you work remotely, S-MM2H if Sarawak suits you. The UK side — a frozen State Pension, FCDO legalisation, HMRC — costs more than most people budget for.

## The half of this decision nobody writes about

If you've spent a weekend reading about MM2H, you've noticed something you probably haven't seen said out loud: every page you found was written by somebody licensed to sell you one programme. The Malaysian half gets copied from the same government guide each time. The British half — what happens to your pension, what HMRC does when you go, which office in London stamps your marriage certificate — doesn't appear anywhere.

So start with the fact that costs the most and gets mentioned the least.

**Your UK State Pension freezes the day you move to Malaysia, and it never catches up.** Annual increases are paid only if you live in the EEA, Switzerland, or a country with a social security agreement with the UK that covers uprating. Malaysia is on none of those lists — it isn't in [the government's list of countries where the increase is paid](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/state-pensions-annual-increases-if-you-live-abroad/countries-where-we-pay-an-annual-increase-in-the-state-pension). Retire to Malaysia at 66 on today's rate and you are still on today's rate at 86, while a friend who stayed in Kent has had twenty years of increases. Over a long retirement that gap is larger than the entire cost of the visa.

That isn't a reason not to go. It's a reason to run the numbers with it in them, which no brochure will do for you.

## Start here: you already have 90 days

British citizens don't need a visa to visit Malaysia. You'll normally be given 90 days on arrival, your passport needs at least six months' validity, and you must submit the free Malaysia Digital Arrival Card online before you travel — airlines check it at the desk. ((Source: FCDO travel advice for Malaysia, entry requirements section, checked 11 August 2026.))

That matters because it reframes the question. You're not asking "how do I get into Malaysia". You're asking how to stay past 90 days without leaving every three months — and, usually, how to buy property and keep a bank account here.

Four routes do that. One of them is yours.

## Which one is actually yours

### You're retired or semi-retired, and you want a base

You're 50-something or older, living on pensions, dividends and rent. You want somewhere warm, English-speaking, with private hospitals you'd trust and a house you own.

**MM2H Silver.** Five years, renewable, no minimum stay once you're 50 or over ((Under 50: {{mm2h-silver:minStayPerYear:text}})). A fixed deposit of {{mm2h-silver:fixedDeposit:money}} pledged with a Malaysian bank, a property purchase from {{mm2h-silver:propertyPurchaseMin:money}}, a government participation fee of {{mm2h-silver:participationFee.principal:money}}, and a government-set agency fee of {{mm2h-silver:agencyFee.principal:money}}.

The honest downsides:

- **You cannot work.** Not consulting, not a directorship, not a side business. Silver and Gold both bar business activity and employment outright. If you plan to keep taking UK client work from a laptop in Penang, this is the wrong pass and people do get caught — Immigration has been enforcing social-visit-pass work violations hard this year.
- **The property floor is not the programme's floor.** {{mm2h-silver:propertyStateFloorNote:text}}
- **The deposit is not your deposit.** Half becomes withdrawable only once your property purchase has completed — so it can't fund the purchase, and it can't be spent on the house afterwards either. Education, medical and tourism in Malaysia only. ((This corrects MOTAC's own December 2025 guide, which reads as though the money frees up earlier and can go towards a residence. Source: MYPVIP practice, as at 3 August 2026.))
- **Adult children age out at 34.** A dependent child must be under 35 — and unmarried. If yours turns 35 mid-application, they're out, so raise it on day one.

There's no published income threshold for MM2H at all. In practice you will still be asked to evidence means: MYPVIP's own March 2026 checklist pre-screens at RM20,000 a month or RM1 million in liquid assets, assessed case by case. ((No income figure appears in MOTAC's published guide. This is agency pre-screening practice, not a stated rule.))

### You're still earning, and you want to keep working

You run a business, you're a partner, you have income that hasn't stopped. You don't want a visa that forbids work.

**PVIP.** {{pvip:tenureYears:years}}, full work and business rights, no minimum stay. You need income of {{pvip:incomeRequirement:moneyPer}} — around £6,700 a month at RM6 to the pound ((Indicative only; the requirement is set in ringgit and the rate moves.)) — a fixed deposit of {{pvip:fixedDeposit:money}}, and a one-off participation fee of {{pvip:participationFee.principal:money}}.

What the older write-ups get wrong, and what matters to a British applicant: the income doesn't have to be salary. Realised investment gains, rental income and pension drawdown all count. So a 58-year-old who sold a company and lives off a portfolio can qualify on PVIP even with no employer — while being told, wrongly, all over the internet that they need a payslip. ((The 2026 terms took effect 16 March 2026. Immigration's published FAQ still shows the 2022 launch terms in places; these figures rest on MYPVIP practice and are dated on the [PVIP guide page](/visas/pvip/).))

The honest downside is the price. The principal has no shorter, cheaper term — it's 20 years at {{pvip:participationFee.principal:money}} or nothing. Add the pass fee of {{pvip:passFeePerYear.principal:money}} per person per year, collected for the whole approved term up front, and the government side alone runs past RM210,000 before your deposit.

### You're under 50 and your work travels

You consult, you write code, you run an agency from a laptop, and your clients are in London.

**DE Rantau.** Three to twelve months, renewable once, income from {{de-rantau:incomeRequirement:moneyPer}} for tech professions ((Non-tech professions must show USD60,000 a year. Source: MDEC's DE Rantau FAQ.)) and no deposit at all. It's the cheapest legal way to work from Malaysia and the shortest — treat it as a two-year test drive, not a plan. Plenty of British applicants use it to live a year in KL before committing RM1,000,000 to a fixed deposit.

### You'd rather Borneo, and a smaller cheque

**Sarawak MM2H.** Age 30 up, income from {{smm2h:incomeRequirement:moneyPer}}, a fixed deposit of {{smm2h:fixedDeposit:money}} — a third of federal Silver in ringgit terms — and 30 cumulative days a year in Sarawak. Property purchase is optional rather than required, which is the real saving. The catch is geography: it's a Sarawak pass, so Kuching or Miri, not KL, and it doesn't give you the peninsula.

### So, the fork

Ask one question: **will you earn money while you're here?**

If yes, and you're under 50 with foreign clients, DE Rantau. If yes, and you're earning RM40,000 a month, PVIP — it's the only route that lets you work and the only one that runs 20 years. If no, you're choosing between federal MM2H Silver and Sarawak, and that's a question about where you want to live, not about money. If you're still earning but under the PVIP income bar and over 50, take MM2H and stop working — or don't take either yet.

Two side-by-sides worth reading before you commit: [MM2H vs PVIP vs DE Rantau](/insights/comparisons/mm2h-vs-pvip-vs-de-rantau/) and [MM2H Platinum vs PVIP](/insights/comparisons/mm2h-platinum-vs-pvip/). You can also run your own numbers on the [eligibility check](/tools/eligibility/) and the [cost calculator](/tools/cost-calculator/).

## The paperwork, from a British address

The document list is short. Getting each piece into a form Malaysia accepts is what takes the weeks.

**What's asked for:** passport copies with every Malaysian entry and exit stamp, and validity of more than two years — not the six months you need to fly. Marriage certificate if your spouse is coming. Birth certificates for children and parents. A letter of good conduct for the principal and every dependant over 18. Bank statements: three months for MM2H, three months or twelve for PVIP depending on which income test you use, certified by the bank or a notary. Passport photos on a white background. PVIP also wants a CV. ((MYPVIP application checklists, MM2H v2 March 2026 and PVIP v2.0 February 2026.))

**Your letter of good conduct is an ACRO Police Certificate.** [ACRO Criminal Records Office](https://www.acro.police.uk/) issues it — that's the UK document Malaysia is asking for when the checklist says "issued by the officials where the applicant is currently residing". Apply online; there's a standard service and a faster premium one. Order it early, because it's the item that expires and the one your spouse forgets they need too.

**And here's the trap: an apostille is not enough.** Malaysia is not a party to the Hague Apostille Convention. ((Checked against the HCCH status table for the 1961 Convention, 11 August 2026 — Malaysia is not among the contracting parties.)) A British document therefore needs the full three-step chain:

1. **Notarised** by a UK notary public.
2. **Legalised by the FCDO** — the [Legalisation Office](https://www.gov.uk/get-document-legalised) service.
3. **Legalised again by the High Commission of Malaysia in London**, 45–46 Belgrave Square. UK-issued documents must carry that final stamp before they're usable in Malaysia.

Budget two to three weeks for the chain per batch, and do it once for everything rather than per document as you discover each requirement. Anything not in English or Malay must also be translated — the Malaysian side quotes around RM180 a page for translation done to its standard.

## The UK side nobody quotes you for

- **Tell HMRC you've gone.** Non-residence isn't a decision you make, it's a test you pass — the [Statutory Residence Test](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rdr3-statutory-residence-test-srt) counts your days and ties. File a P85 when you leave and keep your travel records; see [tax if you leave the UK](https://www.gov.uk/tax-right-retire-abroad-return-to-uk).
- **UK rental income stays UK-taxable.** Keeping the house in Guildford and letting it? That income is taxed in the UK regardless of where you live, under the [non-resident landlord scheme](https://www.gov.uk/tax-uk-income-live-abroad/rent) — your agent or tenant may have to withhold basic-rate tax unless HMRC approves you to receive it gross.
- **You won't be taxed twice.** The [UK–Malaysia double taxation treaty](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/malaysia-tax-treaties) allocates the taxing rights; get advice on your pension specifically, because different pension types are treated differently.
- **Malaysia's side is kinder than you'd expect.** You become tax resident at 182 days in a calendar year, and foreign income you bring in is exempt to 31 December 2036 under P.U.(A) 234/2022 as extended by P.U.(A) 451/2024 — you still declare it and claim the exemption. Full detail: [is foreign income taxed in Malaysia?](/insights/expat-living/is-foreign-income-taxed-in-malaysia/)
- **NHS entitlement ends when you stop being ordinarily resident.** Malaysia has no reciprocal healthcare agreement with the UK, so private cover is the plan — and it's a condition of the visa anyway. Over-60s are exempt from the medical insurance requirement on both MM2H and PVIP; under-60s are not.
- **Two small charges are set by your nationality.** The multiple-entry visa fee is rated by country, and the UK isn't on the published list, so the unlisted rate of RM20 a year applies. The one-off security bond for a British applicant falls in the RM1,500 band. On MM2H the principal's bond is already inside the agency fee. ((MYPVIP fee schedules, March 2026 and February 2026.))

## How long it actually takes

For MM2H: documents pre-screened, engagement letter signed, 20% of the agency fee paid, then **three to four months** waiting for conditional approval. On approval the remaining 80% and the government fees fall due within 90 days, and you fly over to pledge the deposit, do a medical consultation and collect the visa — allow five working days in KL, and give your agent three weeks' notice to get the endorsement appointment.

For PVIP: **two to three months** to approval, government fees within 60 days of it, and six months to make the endorsement trip.

Both mean the same thing in practice: start about a year before you want to be living there, and start the ACRO certificate and the legalisation chain first, because they're the parts you don't control.
